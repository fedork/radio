A learned ranker for state -> winning cut, 2026-08-20
Status: measured. Reproduce with `CENSUS_CACHE=/tmp/c.pkl .venv/bin/python tools/ml/cut_ranker.py`
(~40 s; needs numpy + scikit-learn in the gitignored .venv -- see tools/ml/README.md).

THE FRAMING MATTERS MORE THAN THE MODEL.  "624 forced states" is the wrong way to count this.  The
unit is (state, candidate cut) and the label is "is this cut a winner"; the census enumerates
*every* winner of every endpoint, so unrecorded cap-feasible cuts are clean negatives.  That gives
26,876 positives and effectively unlimited negatives -- about 1e7 cap-feasible cuts per state.  The
scarce axis is states (2,320 four-part across both corpora), and that is what bounds generalization.

WHAT IS BEING PREDICTED, AND AGAINST WHAT DENOMINATOR.  Selectivity is meaningless without a stated
candidate set.  Candidates here are *stage 2*: the information cap on all three children, plus every
part of every child inside the proven per-part bound from data/pareto_sb.csv.  That per-part bound
is itself a sound, cheap filter and it is worth measuring on its own:

  100% of winners pass it, against 8.4% (k=7) and 10.9% (k=8) of cap-feasible candidates
  => a sound ~9-12x filter at full recall, before any learning.

Everything below is the gain *on top of* that.

=====================================================================================
1. HEADLINE: trained on k=7 only, tested on forced k=8 states
=====================================================================================
Training: all 534 four-part k=7 endpoints, 2,995 positives, 323,395 rows.
Test: 120 forced (single-solution) four-part k=8 endpoints, 6,000 stage-2 candidates each.
Neither the state nor the level was seen in training.
Metric: rank candidates by score, try until a winner is hit.  Blind order costs about (N+1)/(P+1).

  ranker                                  median cost   median gain   worst   censored
  logistic regression, 26 features             7           428.6x      6.5x     12%
  gradient boosting, 26 features              11           272.8x      4.1x     10%
  PERMUTED-LABEL CONTROL                    1908             1.6x      0.5x      1%

A median of SEVEN candidates tried before hitting the winner, out of 6,000 sound-filtered ones.
The permuted-label control -- identical pipeline, labels shuffled -- collapses to 1.6x, which is
what rules out leakage; a result this large should not be believed without it.

Note the worst case: 6.5x.  This is a RANKER, not a filter.  It cannot prune (no recall guarantee),
but reordering the search costs nothing and never loses completeness.  That is the honest use.

Within-level k=8 grouped 5-fold CV agrees: 120x median over all states, 300x over forced states,
though both are censored by the 600-negative sample there.

=====================================================================================
2. IS THE CORPUS TOO SMALL?  NO -- IT SATURATES AT ~26 STATES
=====================================================================================
Same held-out test, varying how many k=7 training states the model sees:

     26 training states   333.4x
     53 training states   428.6x
    133 training states   375.1x
    267 training states   375.1x
    534 training states   428.6x

Flat from the very first point.  The corpus is two orders of magnitude larger than this model needs.
Overfitting is not the binding constraint and never was -- the constraint is the feature set.
Corroborating: plain logistic regression BEATS gradient boosting (428x vs 273x), which is what you
see when the decision surface is smooth and low-dimensional rather than data-starved.

=====================================================================================
3. THE ELICITED RULE — how small can it be?
=====================================================================================
  1 feature   diag_mean                                    9.7x   (median cost 310)
  3 features  diag_mean, pure_gap, max_pure               57.8x   (median cost  52)
  5 features  + diag_max, pureB_top2                      95.3x   (median cost  32)
  8 features  + offhalf_mean, margin, mixed_bigrect      250.0x   (median cost  12)
  26 features                                            428.6x   (median cost   7)

The three-feature model has standardized coefficients diag_mean -2.50, pure_gap -2.31,
max_pure -2.06, i.e. all three "smaller is better".  In words, and this is the algorithm:

  Prefer a split that
    (a) cuts every part close to proportionally      -- a_i/n_i near b_i/m_i
    (b) balances the two pure outcomes               -- selected mass near complement mass
    (c) leaves headroom under the cap in the larger pure child
  and, for the last stretch of performance, keeps the cut near half in each part and avoids a
  single dominant rectangle in the pure children.

Feature means, winners vs stage-2 non-winners, in units of the non-winner standard deviation:

  feature              winners   non-winners   gap
  pureB_top2             0.666      0.815      2.02
  diag_max               0.147      0.328      1.70
  diag_mean              0.083      0.179      1.64
  max_pure_over_cap      0.840      0.916      1.51
  pure_gap_over_cap      0.034      0.166      1.30
  margin_over_cap        0.133      0.038      1.29
  offhalf_mean           0.209      0.273      1.16

=====================================================================================
4. A CORRECTION TO THE 2026-08-20 CUT ANALYSIS
=====================================================================================
`evidence/single_solution_cuts_2026-08-20.txt` says diagonal cuts "carry no signal".  That is true
only for the contrast it measured -- forced endpoints against multi-solution endpoints, which is
winners against *other winners*.  Against NON-winners, diagonality is the strongest single feature
in this whole study: diag_mean alone ranks the winner 9.7x better than blind, and it is the second
largest mean-gap of the 26.  Winners cut at |a/n - b/m| = 0.083 on average where sound-filtered
non-winners sit at 0.179.

So both statements hold and the distinction is the point: geometry does not tell you *which states
are forced*, but it does substantially tell you *which cut wins*.  My earlier framing conflated the
two questions, and the earlier "diagonal cuts are rare and carry no signal" line should be read
strictly as scoped to the forced-vs-unforced contrast.

=====================================================================================
5. WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW
=====================================================================================
* No recall guarantee.  Worst observed case is 6.5x, so this can order a search but never prune one.
  The existing sound filters (per-part Pareto ~9-12x, R_0 16.1x, R_0+pairs 140.4x) keep their role.
* Not composed with R_0 or the cross-part pair condition.  The stage-2 denominator is weaker than
  the four-rectangle frontier those were quoted against, so the numbers are not additive with them
  and no combined claim is made here.
* Tested across two levels only (residual k=5 -> k=6).  Transfer to residual k=7 is the thing that
  would actually matter for k=9 and is untested; the scale-normalized features are designed for it,
  but design is not evidence.
* Median is not worst case.  A search using this must still be prepared to enumerate.
* The negatives are uniformly sampled from the stage-2 set.  A solver enumerating in a structured
  order faces a differently-ordered, not differently-composed, candidate set.
