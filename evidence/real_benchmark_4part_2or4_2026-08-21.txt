Status: measured, one state. Continues real_benchmark_residual_control_2026-08-21.txt and

STATE: `Sb(16:12,17:10,29:5,21:6)@6`, documented (docs/results.md) as a "knife edge": exactly 2
winning splits out of 1,212,971,760 raw combinations -- more extreme than the residual control
(`Sb(29:6,19:9,13:12,36:3)@6`, winner count there not documented, but at least 2 distinct literal
winners are now known: the historical `[8:1,8:4,11:11,19:2]` and this thread's own
`[(14,3),(4,1),(11,10),(19,2)]`).

METHOD: identical to the residual-control benchmark -- exact stage-2 (cap + per-part Pareto bound)
enumeration, `R_0` filter (sound), recursive-V order (trained on matched-sampler k=4,5,6,7 pooled),
then real oracle calls (genuine `canSolveB`, no shortcuts) in that order until all three children
of a candidate are `SOLVABLE`.

RESULT:
  stage-2 candidates: 3,686,536      R_0 survivors: 885,342
  learned order: SUCCESS at candidate #1,373: split = (8:7),(4:2),(12:2),(19:6)

Independently re-verified in a fresh process: mass arithmetic exact (202 + 236 + 195 = 633 =
parent mass), all three children genuinely `SOLVABLE`.

**1,373 is worse than the residual control's 67, and that is the informative part, not a
disappointment.** This state's winner count is documented at exactly 2 (or 2 up to
complementation) against the residual control's unknown-but-presumably-larger count -- an even
sharper needle. Against blind search at each stage: ~322x better than R_0-survivor-blind
(885,342/2), ~1,343x better than stage-2-blind, and (extrapolating to the raw space) on the order
of 4-5 hundred thousand-x better than raw-blind. Still overwhelming in absolute terms, but the
number that matters here -- rank among genuinely comparable hard cases -- is clearly not constant;
it varies with exactly how rare the winner is, which is the whole point of choosing this
population.

=====================================================================================
A REAL COST PROBLEM THAT NEEDS FIXING BEFORE THIS SCALES FURTHER
=====================================================================================
The R_0-filtering pass on this state took an anomalous **3,611 seconds** (60 min) for 3,686,536
candidates -- roughly 15x slower per-candidate than every prior run in this thread (the residual
control did a comparable-sized pass in under 10 minutes). The per-checkpoint timings show why:
regular ~110s/500k-row intervals for the first 3,000,000 rows, then a single **2,964-second** gap
for the last ~500,000. That is almost certainly this Mac going to sleep or being heavily
throttled/swapped mid-run, not the algorithm degrading -- but it was not checked in real time, and
it means the wall-clock figures in this file (and likely in the two prior real-benchmark evidence
files, run on the same machine over the same long session) are not a reliable measure of the
pipeline's true cost. The candidate-count results (43 / 67 / 52 / 1,373) are unaffected -- they
are correct regardless of how long the machine took -- but "how long did this take" claims from
this session's local runs should be treated as upper bounds only, not measurements.

=====================================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SHOW
=====================================================================================
* n=1 in this specific tier so far. One 4-part, documented-2-winner state. The residual control
  (unknown winner count, presumably more than 2) and this state bracket a range; a systematic
  sweep across the census's 131 two-winner and 22 four-winner endpoints, rather than one more
  hand-picked example, is the natural way to actually characterize this tier rather than collect
  isolated points.
* Confirms, on a second and harder 4-part instance, that the composed pipeline finds a real,
  oracle-verified witness well inside a tier that is by construction close to a worst case for any
  ordering method (documented winner count of exactly 2 in ~1.2 billion).
* Does not yet address the "4 winners" half of the requested tier, or attempt the width-bounded
  beam/greedy-incremental generator discussed earlier in this thread.
* Not yet wired into radiobase.c.
