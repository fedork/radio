# Completed-target telemetry and dedicated run9 verifier launch

Status: verifier telemetry implemented and locally gated; dedicated AWS launch follows the clean
commit recorded below.  This file is updated with the immutable launch identifiers after EC2
accepts the instance.

## Why the old display was insufficient

The first full run9 replay reached a coloring barrier containing 2,506,515 k=7 targets.  Its frozen
binary printed the target count before doing the work and printed a result only after the whole
level returned.  The status helper therefore could prove that the process was alive at 1,399% CPU,
but it could not distinguish the first target from the last, a changing cost region from steady
work, or a small pathological tail.

The new counters are proof-neutral.  Workers publish:

* one completed/verified/unverified/budget update per finished target;
* completed counts by k and by part count;
* completed recursion nodes; and
* for an active fact, a coarse node cursor once per 2^20 recursion nodes.

A reporter wakes only when `VERIFY_PROGRESS_SECONDS` is positive.  It emits total/window/EWMA
throughput and explicitly labelled ETA projections, plus the three oldest active facts with state,
age and node cursor.  The actual completion counter is separate from the atomic claim cursor.  A
flat completion count with advancing active-node cursors means deep enumeration; flat cursors and
growing ages identify time below one recursion node, normally the dominance index.  `BATCH_START`
and `BATCH_DONE` are exact even without periodic reporting.

## Right-sizing and cost envelope

The AWS API reported these Oregon on-demand inputs on 2026-08-17:

    type             vCPU  physical cores  threads/core  RAM GiB  sustained GHz  USD/hour
    c8a.4xlarge        16       16               1          32         4.5         0.86216
    c7i.4xlarge        16        8               2          32         3.2         0.71400
    r7iz.4xlarge       16        8               2         128         --          1.48800

`c8a.4xlarge` is offered in us-west-2b.  It supplies sixteen real cores for the measured
sixteen-worker policy, while 32 GiB is still over twenty-five times the ~1.2-GiB measured verifier
peak.  The run uses a 30-GiB encrypted gp3 root volume, a 24-GiB RSS guard, a 12-hour cap on each of
coloring and replay, and an independent 25-hour instance shutdown.  It is on-demand: coloring has
no intra-level checkpoint, so a Spot interruption would discard the entire expensive k=7 batch.

The remote supervisor validates the raw and normalized hashes, runs the verifier regression before
the full input, normalizes and byte-round-trips, colors, replays, requires zero unresolved records,
uploads final compressed material, and stops the instance.  `STATUS` and the last 120 structured
progress records are uploaded once per minute.  A failed upload does not delete the 30-GiB EBS
volume; shutdown behavior is stop, not terminate.
