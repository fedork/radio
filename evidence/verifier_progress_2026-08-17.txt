# Completed-target telemetry and dedicated run9 verifier launch

Status: verifier telemetry implemented and locally gated; the dedicated AWS run from the clean
commit and immutable source bundle recorded below was intentionally stopped in the dominant k=7
coloring batch. Final diagnostics were hash-checked; no colored certificate or replay exists.

## Why the old display was insufficient

The first full run9 replay reached a coloring barrier containing 2,506,515 k=7 targets.  Its frozen
binary printed the target count before doing the work and printed a result only after the whole
level returned.  The status helper therefore could prove that the process was alive at 1,399% CPU,
but it could not distinguish the first target from the last, a changing cost region from steady
work, or a small pathological tail.

The new counters are proof-neutral.  Workers publish:

* one completed/verified/unverified/budget update per finished target;
* completed counts by k and by part count;
* completed recursion nodes; and
* for an active fact, a coarse node cursor once per 2^20 recursion nodes.

A reporter wakes only when `VERIFY_PROGRESS_SECONDS` is positive.  It emits total/window/EWMA
throughput and explicitly labelled ETA projections, plus the three oldest active facts with state,
age and node cursor.  The actual completion counter is separate from the atomic claim cursor.  A
flat completion count with advancing active-node cursors means deep enumeration; flat cursors and
growing ages identify time below one recursion node, normally the dominance index.  `BATCH_START`
and `BATCH_DONE` are exact even without periodic reporting.

## Right-sizing and cost envelope

The AWS API reported these Oregon on-demand inputs on 2026-08-17:

    type             vCPU  physical cores  threads/core  RAM GiB  sustained GHz  USD/hour
    c8a.4xlarge        16       16               1          32         4.5         0.86216
    c7i.4xlarge        16        8               2          32         3.2         0.71400
    r7iz.4xlarge       16        8               2         128         --          1.48800

`c8a.4xlarge` is offered in us-west-2b.  It supplies sixteen real cores for the measured
sixteen-worker policy, while 32 GiB is still over twenty-five times the ~1.2-GiB measured verifier
peak.  The run uses a 30-GiB encrypted gp3 root volume, a 24-GiB RSS guard, a 12-hour cap on each of
coloring and replay, and an independent 25-hour instance shutdown.  It is on-demand: coloring has
no intra-level checkpoint, so a Spot interruption would discard the entire expensive k=7 batch.

The remote supervisor validates the raw and normalized hashes, runs the verifier regression before
the full input, normalizes and byte-round-trips, colors, replays, requires zero unresolved records,
uploads final compressed material, and stops the instance.  `STATUS` and the last 120 structured
progress records are uploaded once per minute.  A failed upload does not delete the 30-GiB EBS
volume; shutdown behavior is stop, not terminate.

Monitor once or continuously:

    tools/run9_verifier_progress_status.sh
    tools/run9_verifier_progress_status.sh --watch

For this exact run, append `20260818T014906Z` to either command.

This transition also exposed that the total and 0.75-history EWMA ETAs react too slowly to an
ordered cost-regime change. The local status helper now adds
`LATEST_WINDOW_PROJECTION ... basis=latest_interval_only` from the frozen run's `rate_window`; at
960 seconds it reported 59.39 days. Future verifier builds emit `eta_window_s` alongside total and
EWMA projections. This post-launch display-only C source has SHA-256
`7664d73b9ac1eeb222c4314e69c203632e35ec8b6616e8db3b5133bdc663c435`; it is not the binary running
on EC2. A one-thread two-root check put the quick `Sb(33:33)@7` before the retained hard root and
printed numeric `eta_window_s=1` while the second task was active. It then verified both roots with
zero unresolved and exactly 4,644,470 nodes in 12.85 seconds, one node more than the hard root alone.

Three bounded bootstrap attempts preceded the accepted run and were terminated after their logs
were preserved.  `i-06b1c144063fc4d88` exposed the AL2023 `curl`/`curl-minimal` package conflict;
`i-09b7e4eb76bc3d414` reached the self-test and exposed its undeclared `rg` dependency; and
`i-04b2cfad884a000a8` showed that `ripgrep` is absent from the enabled AL2023 repositories.  The
final fix leaves the image's existing `curl-minimal` in place and makes the regression portable
with `grep -E`.  The three failures consumed about 21, 34 and 20 seconds before detection and did
not touch the certificate pipeline.

## Final stop, preservation and disposition

At the user's direction, full coloring was deferred: a verifier that independently re-solves the
same negative states more slowly than the proof-producing solver is not a useful full-corpus proof
format. TERM was sent to the exact capped-run wrapper, which propagated it to the verifier and let
the supervisor perform its normal final upload. The final status was:

No interrupted verifier artifact was promoted to a GitHub release. The normalized certificate is
a derived duplicate of the durable run9 raw proof source, and an incomplete color log is diagnostic
rather than proof evidence. The exact measurements and hashes are retained here; the S3 objects
remain available for operational inspection.

The next verifier prototype should validate solver-emitted split-space coverage and lower-level
citations rather than search for that coverage independently. The current verifier remains useful
for small complete corpora, targeted audits and regression tests. Top-down coloring should be
revisited only after checking an explicit derivation is cheap enough that reachability—not proof
search—is the coloring cost.
