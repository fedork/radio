# Last-segment Pareto-cache implementation measurements, 2026-08-10

Scope
-----

Candidate: origin/main 23de2ac plus the compact cache implementation subsequently committed.
Machine: Apple M4 Pro, 24 GB, macOS; all runs single-threaded.
Checkpoint source: sa193-local-2026-08-10:out_sa193.txt, filtered by k without reordering.
The three replay files contain 107,981 k=5, 98,124 k=6, and 6,466 k=7 lines.

The implementation keeps exact prefix traversal but folds the final part into maximal-positive and
minimal-negative 2-D Pareto fronts.  Every child entry is a tagged uint32 descriptor.  Singleton
fronts are inline; non-singletons use small vectors; retained branches use uint32 child arrays.

Verdict regression against the old trie
---------------------------------------

tools/cache_query_regression.c first verifies every exact checkpoint fact, then emits one byte per
targeted easier/harder/prefix/extension mutation and 500,000 deterministic valid random states.
The source was compiled once against the pre-change engine and once against the candidate.  Full
byte comparisons (not hashes) gave:

  k=5: 1,811,621 queries; 3,642 old MAYBE -> new TRUE; no other difference
  k=6: 1,773,102 queries;   935 old MAYBE -> new TRUE; no other difference
  k=7:   580,235 queries;    45 old MAYBE -> new TRUE; no other difference
  total: 4,164,958 queries; 4,622 old MAYBE -> new TRUE; no lost or reversed verdict

The strengthening is expected and sound: an exact positive child folded into a last-part front also
answers componentwise-smaller last parts that the old canonical closure did not always materialise.
Negative answers were required to match exactly.  An early prototype that returned from an entire
negative recursive call lost two old negative mutation hits and was rejected.  The final version
skips only dominated child edges while continuing all other segment permutations.

Other correctness gates:

  * all 1,039 CHECK lines from tools/split_regression.c match the old engine;
  * that corpus and a 10,000-fact replay pass ASan+UBSan;
  * radio_print renders 24 nodes under ASan+UBSan and check_witness.py verifies the tree;
  * main drivers, pairtab, tripletab, quadtab, and fast_replay compile against the new API;
  * MAX_N=258, 336, and 1030 variants pass syntax compilation (large ids use the uint32 fallback).

Exact-state L1 follow-up
------------------------

Profiling the compact build on the same 2,201,187-fact `warm_k5.txt` control attributed roughly
19% of sampled solve time directly to `checkCache`, chiefly the final-front comparisons.  A
pre-front snapshot solved the control in 33.0 CPU seconds; compact commit c146d9d took 42.6 seconds.
Both found `[8:1,8:4,11:11,19:2]` after exactly 37,899 top-level splits.

The deployed follow-up keeps the 32-bit tagged trie and puts a bounded exact-result L1 in front of
it.  At `MAX_N=193`, 65,536 direct-mapped entries occupy exactly 2,097,152 zero-filled bytes.  Each
entry holds the complete normalized sequence for up to 12 parts, its level, a 32-bit fingerprint
and a definitive verdict.  The full sequence and level are compared after the fingerprint, so a
hash collision can only cause a miss.  `MAYBE` is never stored.  Longer states bypass L1 and use
the unchanged theorem/trie path.

Placement was more important than lookup mechanics.  L1 is probed after the information bound,
unit elimination and sorting, but before singleton/full-star majorization.  Thus an exact warm hit
skips both repeated majorization and dominance-trie traversal.  On a miss, singleton
majorization, full-star majorization and the trie retain their prior order.  Definitive results
from any of those sources and from completed search are safe to retain because they are permanent
mathematical facts, unlike a deadline `MAYBE`.

With a 16,384-entry instrumented prototype, the warm control made 708,562,672 normalized queries;
708,562,650 were at most 12 parts and 542,949,678 hit exactly (76.63%).  It stored 61,427,871
definitive misses.  A clean size sweep, with identical witness and split count, gave:

  entries / table bytes       solve CPU
  32,768 / 1,048,576             26.7 s
  65,536 / 2,097,152             26.6 s
 131,072 / 4,194,304             27.1 s

The middle size is the default.  Relative to the matched reruns above, 26.6 seconds is 37.56%
less solve CPU than compact c146d9d and 19.39% less than the pre-front engine.  Whole-process wall,
including the unchanged 2.2-million-line replay, was 48.74 seconds.

Rejected variants are part of the result.  Merely forcing the compact traversal inline improved
42.6 to 39.7 seconds.  Replacing 32-bit branch handles by direct pointers gained less than one
second but raised maximum RSS from roughly 624 to 966 MB, so it was reverted.  Packing each exact
query into 64-bit words made the scalar-hash prototype slower, 37.5 versus 36.1 seconds, and was
also reverted.  Very large direct tables had already lost to cache pressure: 1,048,576 entries
took 38.8 seconds even when the L1 still sat after majorization.

Final correctness gates:

  * Against compact baseline c146d9d, all 3,379,067 verdict bytes from the combined-checkpoint
    regression matched exactly: 543,116 TRUE, 1,819,528 FALSE and 1,016,423 MAYBE.  Both files had
    SHA-256 94bf295ef197107a72d4e545cde05e0fe82d75b465b362071c3906008f0e9f00.
  * All 1,038 definitive answers (1,039 `CHECK` lines including the summary) from
    `tools/split_regression.c` matched c146d9d exactly.
  * That corpus passed AddressSanitizer plus UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer; the legacy `checkCache`
    diagnostic API still changed its test state from MAYBE to FALSE after insertion as expected.
  * `MAX_N=1030` syntax compilation passed.  The packed two-coordinate dominance comparison was
    also checked exhaustively over all 9,312 rectangles at `MAX_N=193`: 86,713,344 ordered pairs,
    zero disagreements with two scalar comparisons.  Larger coordinates use the scalar fallback.

The live local cold run and AWS `run4` use their frozen pre-L1 binaries and were deliberately not
restarted.  These measurements apply to future runs or a later same-log resume on current source;
they do not retroactively change those processes' recorded timings.
