The recursive value/policy predictor: first build and measurement, 2026-08-20
Status: measured. Scripts: tools/ml/recursive_value.py, tools/ml/value_gen_states.py (extended).
Continues evidence/value_level_transfer_2026-08-20.txt and docs/ml-guided-search.md's "First
experiment" spec. Needs numpy + scikit-learn in .venv, and a warm `radio_oracle` for fresh labels
(none of this reads or writes the sa193 cache/snapshot -- these are unrelated synthetic states).

QUESTIONS ASKED, IN ORDER:
1. Does the matched-sampler + oracle-label protocol that worked at k=4->k=5 hold further up the
   ladder, through k=7 -- the level ml-guided-search.md flagged as untested and as where MAYBE
   verdicts start appearing?
2. The design note's diagnosis was "the flat ranker predicts the OR-choice from the parent's shape
   alone and stalls at median rank 76 of 54,014; what determines the winner lives one level down."
   Does scoring a candidate split by the trained value of its own three children -- with ZERO
   supervision on which splits win -- recover ranking power close to a ranker trained directly on
   winning-split labels?

=====================================================================================
0. TWO THINGS BROKE FIRST, BEFORE EITHER QUESTION COULD BE ANSWERED
=====================================================================================

(a) THE FIXED MASS BAND DOES NOT TRANSFER ACROSS LEVELS.
value_level_transfer.py's sampler drew masses uniformly in [0.70,1.02] of cap=3^k, the same band
at every k. At k=7 this produced **0 solvable of 300** decided states. The per-part solo Pareto
maximum (MAXN1 in data/pareto_sb.csv) grows almost as fast as the cap itself with k (e.g. at k=7,
m=2: max n1=127, so a single part alone can already carry 254/2187 = 11.6% of cap; summed over 4
random parts the naive ceiling averages ~90% of cap). So "mass near cap" increasingly means "mass
past what any part-respecting split can carry," not a level-independent notion of difficulty -- a
fixed fraction-of-cap band is a k=4/k=5 artifact, not a sampler property.

Fix: bisect the band per level with the warm oracle itself before drawing the training sample.
`tools/ml/value_gen_states.py` now takes explicit `lo hi` fraction-of-cap arguments (default
[0.70,1.02] preserved for k<=5 reproducibility). The bands a 7-step bisection (30-state probes,
target 50% solved-of-decided) actually chose:

  k=4  [0.897,0.977]   k=5  [0.796,0.876]   k=6  [0.688,0.768]   k=7  [0.480,0.560]

The lower edge falls by roughly 0.1 of cap per level from k=5 on. That the achievable-mass
fraction shrinks with k is not new -- it is the same reason the Pareto columns thin out -- but
this is the first time it broke an ML data pipeline, and it will break the next one that assumes
one band transfers.

(b) A LONG-LIVED WARM ORACLE CAN CRASH OUTRIGHT ON AN ORDINARY QUERY.
Labeling the k=7 sample at the chosen band, the oracle process's pipe closed mid-run six times in
300 queries. Its stderr (previously discarded -- `docs/ml-guided-search.md`'s Oracle usage sends
it to DEVNULL by default) read:

  can't solve Sb(36:4,55:2,36:3,22:3,63:1,13:3,37:1,3:3)[576,285] in 6 ... totalsplits=6312401 ...
  out of front-record handles

`alloc_front_record` in radiobase.c hard-caps the handle count at `NODE_HANDLE_MASK = 0x3fffffff`
(~1.07e9) because the handle is packed into a tagged 32-bit descriptor; `front_records_len` only
grows across the process's lifetime, and a handful of multi-million-split sub-searches (residual
k=6 states reached inside a single k=7 query) can burn through a meaningful fraction of that space
before the run ever gets near the theoretical 1e9 cap in aggregate over hundreds of queries. This
is the same category of trap already on record for `radio_canon_search_generic`'s node pool
("allocates 1.26 GB per pool chunk and never frees one") but had not been observed or documented
for the warm-oracle workflow, which is exactly the usage `ml-guided-search.md` recommends for
"everything that needs many verdicts." No workaround at compile time is documented for this
driver (unlike `radio_canon_search_generic`'s `MAX_TREE_NODES`/`MAX_MEMO`).

Mitigation used here, not a fix: label with a wrapper (kept at /tmp/rec/label_resilient.py, not
committed -- it is a two-line retry loop, reproducible from this note) that catches the closed
pipe, logs the offending state, restarts the oracle, and skips that one query. 6 of 300 states at
k=7 are lost this way (recorded as "CRASH", loaded as neither class). The oracle's own budget
command bounds the deterministic accepted-prefix clock, not this: a state can be rejected by
budget while still allocating unboundedly many front-records on the way to the deadline.

Also observed in the same run: the nominal-second budget (5 here) does not tightly bound wall
time. Individual queries took 243.9 s, 270.5 s and 215.8 s of real time against a 5-nominal-second
budget, because the clock counts accepted split prefixes, not all explored work -- consistent with
the documented budget semantics, just a sharper illustration of the gap than seen before.

=====================================================================================
1. LEVEL-HELD-OUT VALUE MODEL, EXTENDED TO k=7
=====================================================================================
Protocol per docs/ml-guided-search.md's "First experiment": matched sampler (same generator, same
mass band choice procedure, oracle-labelled) for every level; permuted-label control; held-out
level, not a random split. 300 states drawn at each level's bisected band; MAYBE and CRASH labels
dropped (never recorded as either class). Features: the pooled, permutation- and part-count-
invariant vector from value_level_transfer.py (sum/mean/max/min/std/median of per-part area, and
of n, m, aspect ratio, each normalized by cap or sqrt(cap); plus the per-part Pareto deficit).

  train k=4: 300 states, 41.7% solvable        train k=5: 300, 64.0%
  train k=6: 294, 35.0%                        test  k=7: 273, 45.1%

  train->test              mass/cap   deficit(sound)   logistic   boosted   permuted-control
  k=4 -> k=5                0.5607        0.9284         0.9778    0.9531       0.4086
  k<=5 -> k=6                0.6154        0.9710         0.9938    0.9655       0.6383
  k<=6 -> k=7                0.5923        0.9961         0.9861    0.9956       0.4821

Transfer holds all the way to k=7 -- but the sound per-part deficit baseline is now essentially as
strong as either learned model (0.9961), which it was not at k=4/k=5 (0.9284). **The learned
model's aggregate-AUC edge over the cheap sound filters shrinks as k grows towards where it
matters.** The honest subset is what neither sound filter decides:

  k=7 HARD SUBSET: 131 of 273 states (93.9% solvable)     mass/cap 0.8430   boosted 0.9705

A real, if narrower, edge survives on exactly the states worth spending a learned model on.

Caveat on the k<=5->k=6 permuted control: 0.6383, well above the ~0.41-0.48 seen at the other two
levels. Still far below the real models' 0.99+, so not the corpus-identity failure the trap table
already warns about (that one showed AUC ~0.95 on a control that also collapsed to ~0.5) -- but a
reminder that a single-shuffle control on ~600 training rows is itself noisy. Worth a second shuffle
before leaning on this specific comparison.

=====================================================================================
2. RECURSIVE CUT SCORING: V(child) ONE LEVEL DOWN, ON REAL CENSUS SPLITS
=====================================================================================
The design note's diagnosis, tested directly: for every stage-2 candidate cut of a real, forced
(single-winning-class) k=7 census endpoint, compute its three actual AND-OR children -- selected,
mixed, complement, via analyze_single_solution_cuts.children -- and score the cut by
min(V(selected), V(mixed), V(complement)), using V trained ONLY on the synthetic matched sampler
above (k<=6; zero exposure to any split/cut label, ever). Same population, same per-endpoint
negative sampling (per_endpoint=6000), same cost/ratio metric as cut_ranker.py's evaluate(), so
the numbers below are directly comparable to that script's own output.

For a fair baseline, cut_ranker's flat shape ranker was retrained on k8 (instead of its usual k7)
and evaluated on the SAME 153 forced k7 endpoints used below -- this flat ranker WAS trained with
direct supervision on which splits win, unlike the recursive scorer:

  153 forced k7 endpoints, median candidate-set size 6,001 (stage-2: info cap + per-part bound)

  ranker                                          median cost   median ratio   worst
  flat shape, trained k8 WITH cut supervision          23           130.5x      4.5x
  recursive logistic-V, ZERO cut supervision           25           120.0x      1.9x
  recursive gradient-boosted-V, ZERO cut supervision  1331             2.3x      0.6x

**The recursive logistic-V scorer, which never saw a single winning-split label, comes within 8%
of a flat ranker directly supervised on winning splits, on genuinely held-out real census data.**
This is the clearest evidence yet for the design note's central claim: recursion captures most of
what the flat shape ranker needs cut-level supervision to approximate. It is also a real
distribution-shift test in the sense evidence/value_level_transfer_2026-08-20.txt's section 4
flagged as untested: V here was trained on synthetic random states and evaluated on genuine
children of real solver splits, a distribution neither the sampler nor `V` were built to match --
and it held up anyway.

**Gradient-boosted V collapses under recursion despite winning on standalone AUC.** Table 1 above
has boosted V ahead of logistic V at the k=7 holdout (0.9956 vs 0.9861); composed recursively and
applied under distribution shift, it is worse than blind on the hardest endpoint (0.6x) and near-
useless on the median (2.3x vs logistic's 120.0x). Consistent with this repo's repeated finding
that logistic regression's smooth decision surface generalizes better than boosting's histogram
steps (cut_ranker, value_level_transfer) -- this shows the gap matters far more once a model is
composed (min over three children) and shifted out of its training distribution than it does for
flat in-distribution classification. **Standalone AUC is not a safe guide to which model to use
recursively; only the composed, end-to-end metric is.**

=====================================================================================
3. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT LICENSE
=====================================================================================
* The recursive scorer is ready to prototype in front of the solver's split loop, per
  ml-guided-search.md step 3 (only reached once the level-holdout separated -- it did). That C
  integration is NOT done in this session: it is a correctness-sensitive change to the split loop,
  and per the achievability/unsolvability asymmetry already on record, must only ever REORDER
  achievability search, never prune an OR-branch.
* The 120x/130.5x numbers are the same "candidates tried before hitting a winner" selectivity
  metric cut_ranker.py already uses, not measured solver CPU seconds. Converting selectivity into
  an end-to-end speedup still requires costing V's own evaluation against the sound filters it
  would run alongside (mass/cap and the per-part bound are one array lookup each; V is a ~31-
  feature logistic regression -- cheap, but not free, and the exact cost has not been measured
  against actual solver internals).
* This was tested at children-at-k=6 only (scoring real k7 endpoint splits). The k8 -> children-
  at-k7 case is untested here and is the one that hits both open traps above (k=7 sampling needs
  the bisected band, and k=7 sub-searches are the ones observed to crash the oracle) at once.
* Reproduce: build `/tmp/rec/oracle_k7` at `-DMAX_K=7 -DMAX_N=350` (cold, no cache -- these are
  independent synthetic states, not sa193 facts), bisect+label per level with a resilient wrapper
  (retry-on-closed-pipe, log and skip the offending state), then
  `.venv/bin/python tools/ml/recursive_value.py`. The matched-sampler data itself lives under
  /tmp/rec/labeled3_k{4,5,6,7}.txt and is not committed (small, and exactly reproducible from the
  fixed RNG seed `1000+k` in value_gen_states.py given the same bands and oracle build/commit).
