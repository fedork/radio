# Canonical cut-and-splice survey (`K<=3`, with `K=4` probes; 2026-08-29)

## Question

Can the global singleton construction be made simpler by preserving the shape of the canonical
Pascal chains?  The tested normal form is:

1. cut every canonical chain into contiguous intervals in Pascal-rank order;
2. make every target chain from either one interval or two intervals;
3. when two intervals are joined, take them from canonical chains of different Pascal depths,
   require their rank ranges to be disjoint, and check that their union is a chain.

Every canonical vertex is used exactly once.  Thus a successful exact cover is itself a valid
chain partition of the transcript poset, not merely a numerical size decomposition.

## Method

`tools/singleton_cut_splice_survey.py` constructs the canonical chains as ternary transcripts and
enumerates every nonempty contiguous interval.  It tests a proposed union using the exact
first-differing-symbol conflict rule for `Q_K`.  For each full-mass partition dominated by `G_K`,
an exact-cover search then partitions all `3^K` words into candidates having precisely the target
part sizes.

The target enumeration is independent of the candidate generation.  A `PASS` therefore provides
an explicit valid partition.  A `FAIL` exhausts the stated restricted candidate family; it is not
a claim that the singleton state is unsolvable.

## Exact results

There are respectively 2, 15, and 1,206 full-mass dominated types at `K=1,2,3`.

| level | types obtainable by cuts alone | types obtainable in the two-interval normal form |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 2 / 2 | 2 / 2 |
| 2 | 11 / 15 | 15 / 15 |
| 3 | 591 / 1,206 | 1,206 / 1,206 |

At `K=3` there are 88 single-chain intervals.  The following restrictions isolate what the
successful splices need.

| allowed candidates | candidate count | result over all 1,206 targets |
|---|---:|---|
| two compatible intervals from distinct source chains | 839 | pass |
| additionally, distinct Pascal depths | 827 | pass |
| additionally, disjoint rank ranges | 821 | pass |
| additionally, depth gap at most two | 752 | pass |
| depth gap exactly one | 474 | fail at `(8,3,3,3,3,3,3,1)` |
| shallower-depth interval always below the deeper one | 698 | fail at `(8,7,3,3,3,1,1,1)` |
| deeper-depth interval always below the shallower one | 211 | fail at `(8,7,4,3,2,1,1,1)` |

Cuts alone first fail at `(4,2,2,1)` for `K=2` and at `(8,7,4,3,2,1,1,1)` for `K=3`.
Consequently splicing is not cosmetic.  Conversely, neither same-depth splices nor interleaved
rank ranges are needed at `K=3`.  The splice direction in rank order must be chosen adaptively,
and an induction restricted to neighboring Pascal depths is too rigid.

The balanced target `(3^9)` has a particularly transparent normal-form cover:

```text
(010,001 | 002)   (102,012 | 000)   (100,011 | 022)
(110,101 | 202)   (210,201,200)     (112,121 | 222)
(111 | 021,020)   (122,212,221)     (120,211 | 220)
```

Each vertical bar is the sole splice in that target chain; entries on either side are contiguous
in their respective canonical source chains.  The two unbarred triples are single canonical
intervals.  Notice that `(102,012 | 000)` joins a depth-two interval below a depth-zero interval,
opposite to `(010,001 | 002)`, which joins depth zero below depth three.  This illustrates why a
fixed rank direction fails even though strict source-depth orientation remains available.

## `K=4` probes

The same normal form has 456 canonical intervals and 20,542 candidates at `K=4`.  Exact covers
were found for the canonical profile, the transfer-shaped target

    (16,15,11,9,7,5,5,5,1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1),

and the balanced targets `(5^16,1)`, `(4^20,1)`, and `(3^27)`.

A seeded sample of 100 targets generated by 1--60 random Robin--Hood transfers from `G_4` found
normal-form covers for 85.  The remaining 15 reached the per-target cap of 20,000 search nodes;
none was exhaustively rejected.  These capped cases are inconclusive and the sample is not a
`K=4` theorem.

## Reproduction

From the repository root:

```text
tools/singleton_cut_splice_survey.py
tools/singleton_cut_splice_survey.py --k4-samples --k4-random 100
```

The exact `K<=3` run used 208,907 search nodes for the strongest passing normal form and took 3.2
seconds for that variant on the local machine.  The seeded `K=4` sample used 340,651 nodes across
all targets, passed 85, capped 15, failed zero, and took 49.4 seconds.  The script is
dependency-free.

## Conclusion and proof target

The finite data suggests the following sufficient strengthening of singleton majorization.

> **Pascal Two-Interval Splicing Conjecture.** For every `a<=_w G_K`, the canonical Pascal chains
> can be cut into intervals and repartitioned into chains of sizes `a`, with every target chain
> consisting of at most two rank-separated compatible intervals from distinct canonical depths.

This would avoid a transfer schedule entirely.  Orient every two-source splice from smaller to
larger Pascal depth; the source-depth graph is then acyclic.  The remaining construction is a
global cut-and-matching problem: choose the interval endpoints and match fragments whose two
boundary endpoints are comparable in the Pascal code poset.  This is substantially narrower than
arbitrary recoloring, but it is still conjectural beyond `K=3` and it does not by itself prove the
Canonical Monotone-Transfer Conjecture, because a final cut-and-splice partition need not arise
through direct unit moves.
