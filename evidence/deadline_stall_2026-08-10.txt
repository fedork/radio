# Sa(192) control stall: debugger reconstruction and regression, 2026-08-10

Scope
-----

The affected cold sessions were AWS `run4` (compact cache, frozen bundle `6af384e`) and
`run5` (compact cache plus exact L1, frozen commit `290a892`).  Both remained runnable on one
core with a small, stable footprint, but stopped writing verdicts before the mandatory Sa(192)
control returned.  A missing control line is not a verdict; this file records an engine liveness
bug, not a mathematical result.

Debugger evidence
-----------------

matched the running binaries byte-for-byte:

  run4 live/debug SHA-256  6606d606b5a848927391df65a3b24041d4fa880fa54c42121d2098e802bfa25c
  run5 live/debug SHA-256  f3ea042b0b9b2378b52a1a016c1a965bf71e2bcc33e41894401b0cd6f9cee744

Cause and repair
----------------

The old complete-candidate block wrapped the clock/deadline test in

  cant_solve_count >= cant_solve_count_min

so a search producing no new negative verdict could never observe its expired deadline.  There
was a second liveness hole: while the count equalled the minimum, every visit recomputed
`deadline = max(deadline, now + 5*(now-start))`; a single new negative could therefore slide the
deadline indefinitely.  Finally, the clock was sampled only when a complete long-state candidate
survived every prefix filter, so even a corrected leaf check could overshoot badly when leaves
were sparse.

The repair makes the deadline independent of negative progress.  The first observed negative may
grant the existing five-times-elapsed grace once per `canSolveB` call.  Bounded searches also poll
every 65,536 admitted prefix candidates; unbounded roots retain their existing iterative behaviour.
A deadline return is `MAYBE`, never `FALSE`, so the change cannot manufacture a refutation.

Exact before/after replay
-------------------------

The retained run4 checkpoint was 3,684,161 bytes with SHA-256
`2a5dfb8014305f6f22fc8420bf219f6bc07a56258362ae2a9917a0039594960d`.  A scratch driver loaded
it, asked the reconstructed state at k=5, and supplied a finite five-second parent budget.  Both
binaries used `MAX_K=10`, `MAX_N=193`, and `clang -O3`.

Frozen run4 engine:

  [capped_run] deadline-old: TIMEOUT after 12s | exit 124 | wall 17s | peak RSS 0.24 GB

Patched engine:

  REACH: 12782758 tested, 2253279 pruned (17.6%)
  PROBE result=2 cpu=3.002 negatives=0
  [capped_run] deadline-fixed: completed | exit 0 | wall 10s | peak RSS 0.23 GB

`result=2` is `MAYBE`.  The 3.002-second return matches the configured minimum child budget and,
critically, occurs with zero new negatives.  The 12.8 million prefixes in three CPU seconds also
show why sampling every 2^16 prefixes is cheap and why waiting only for complete leaves was weak.

  * The cache-query corpus emitted 4,164,958 verdict bytes with the established counts:
    k=5 1,811,621; k=6 1,773,102; k=7 580,235.  Concatenated SHA-256 was
    `2b1e91a4a5855736e1aee15eb89a05c7fb2cd6bbaeae05b00c3c9aee23ab64e2`.
  * `tools/split_regression.c` emitted 1,038 answers plus its summary (1,039 `CHECK` lines),
    and optimized output matched ASan+UBSan output byte-for-byte.
  * `radio_sa193.c` syntax-compiles at `MAX_N=1030`.
  * Loading the active local run's own checkpoint reproduced the mandatory positive control from
    cache: `result CONTROL Sa(192) in 10 = SOLVABLE`.

Run disposition
---------------

After the state and cause were independently reproduced, run4 and run5 no longer had diagnostic
value.  Their wrappers were terminated at 2026-08-11 05:37:59 UTC by SSM command
`ab7765b7-00da-4c3f-a94d-48c785947251`; both solver processes exited, while run3 and its watchdog
remained alive.  Their own watchdogs were left running to make final immutable S3 uploads.  This is
an aborted-engine record, not evidence for or against Sa(192) or Sa(193).
