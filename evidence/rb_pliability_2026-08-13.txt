# Hereditary suffix pliability for joint reachability

Status: diagnostic theorem and measurements, 2026-08-13.  No production pruning, split ordering or
`RB_TRIGGER` policy was changed.  `tools/rb_root_probe.c` owns the opt-in analysis; ordinary solver
drivers compile the existing `radiobase.c` path without calling it.

## Exact question

Work with the reduced state after Unit Group Elimination, so its `P` parts are all nonunit.  At level
`k`, let one child hold at most `C=3^(k-1)` pair candidates, let the reduced state mass be `M`, and
write the **absolute slack** as

```
sigma = 3*C - M.
```

For suffix `i`, let `W_i` be its mass.  After some prefix has been assigned, its residual capacities
`h=(h0,h1,h2)` satisfy

```
0 <= hj <= C,             h0+h1+h2 = W_i+sigma.
```

Call the suffix `sigma`-pliable if *every* such capacity triple admits some retained suffix routing
`r` with `rj<=hj`.  This is deliberately universal over capacity triples; a nonpliable suffix says
only that `rb_dead` has something it could reject, not that the obstructing triple is reachable from
an actual search prefix.

The existing `rb_mx` table decides this definition without another reachability construction.  Since
`r1=W_i-r0-r2`, suffix `i` is pliable exactly when, for every admissible `h0,h2` (and hence `h1`),

```
rb_mx[i][h0,h2] >= W_i-h1.
```

The diagnostic scans all suffixes and then scans backward.  `exact_head=i` means that suffix `i` and
every suffix after it are pliable, so the first `i` parts are the rigid head and the remaining
`P-i` parts are a hereditary pliable tail.  Production calls `rb_dead` only with suffix indices
`1..P-1`; `potential_call_suffixes=0` therefore proves that its per-prefix lookup is vacuous for the
whole state.  It does not decide the recursive solvability of any child.

## Cheap sufficient certificates

The report compares the exact cutoff with two one-sided certificates.  They inspect only masses and
whether the ordinary theorem-filtered split tables retained the required routings.

1. If `sigma>=2*C`, then every residual capacity is at least the entire suffix mass: the other two
   capacities total at most `2*C`.  Any retained routing fits, so every suffix is pliable.
2. A retained `(2:1)` part has mass routings `(2,0,0)`, `(0,2,0)`, `(0,0,2)`, `(1,1,0)` and
   `(0,1,1)`.  At `sigma>=1` this is a pliable one-part base.
3. If a tail of mass `T` is pliable and a preceding part of mass `w` retains all three pure corner
   routings, the extended tail is pliable whenever

   ```
   2*w <= T+sigma+2.
   ```

   Indeed, that inequality forces at least one residual capacity to be at least `w`; route the new
   part wholly there and apply tail pliability to the reduced capacities.
4. For an ascending tail of `q` nonunit masses, let `D=W-2q` be its excess above `q` copies of
   `(2:1)`.  With the same retained-corner premise, the simpler corollary is `q>=D+2` at `sigma=1`,
   or `q>=D+1` at `sigma>=2`.  The direct extension test is stronger; the report labels this coarse
   corollary separately.

For the corollary, write `w_i=2+d_i` with nondecreasing nonnegative integer `d_i` and `d_1=0`.
At extension step `i`, the direct condition becomes

```
2*d_i <= 2*i + (d_1+...+d_(i-1)) + sigma - 4.                 (1)
```

Put `b=2` for `sigma=1` and `b=1` for `sigma>=2`; the respective length hypotheses say
`D<=q-b`.  If `d_i>0`, monotonicity gives

```
(q-i+1)*d_i <= D <= q-b,
```

and hence `(q-i+1)(d_i-1)<=i-1-b`, so `d_i<=i-b`.  For `b=2`, this makes the left side of (1) at
most `2i-4`, below its `2i-3` right side even before the earlier excess is added.  For `b=1`, it is
at most `2i-2`, which is the right side at `sigma=2` before earlier excess.  The `d_i=0` steps are
immediate.  Thus every extension succeeds, and the one-part `(2:1)` base closes the induction.

The corner premise matters.  A pure routing leaves the original part in one child, and the split
table removes it when that child fails the information or full-star-majorization filter at `k-1`.
The diagnostic inspects the retained table rather than assuming corners from raw combinatorics.

```

```

## Measurements

The two sharp synthetic controls behave as predicted.

- Thirteen `(2:1)` parts at `k=3` have `M=26`, `C=9`, `sigma=1`.  Every one of the thirteen
  nonempty suffixes is exactly pliable; both certificates give `head=0, tail=13`.
- `(3:1)` plus twelve `(2:1)` parts has `M=27`, hence `sigma=0`.  The root itself packs exactly
  `9/9/9`, but the last `(2:1)` suffix is not pliable.  The hereditary tail is empty and nine of the
  twelve suffix indices queried by production `rb_dead` are potentially useful.  Thus root-ALIVE
  and hereditary pliability are genuinely different tests.

The complete prior k=3 census has 283 four-part states.  Parent full-star majorization rejects 40;
the probe analyzes the remaining 243 and its existing root test rejects 5.  Among those 243:

```
exact_head distribution:       0:64, 1:1, 2:58, 3:40, 4:80
direct-theorem head:            1:29, 2:34, 3:45, 4:135
coarse q/D head:                1:10, 2:25, 3:51, 4:157
no callable suffix can prune:   exact 65, direct theorem 29, coarse theorem 10
```

The exact no-call count rises with absolute slack but is not determined by it:

```
sigma          0       1       2       3       4       5
analyzed      23      45      45      49      39      42
exact no-call  0       3      11      13      15      23
theorem        0       1       2       5       9      12
```

The diagnostic asserts `coarse => direct theorem => exact hereditary` at every suffix; all 243
analyzed states pass.  The direct theorem nevertheless recognizes only 29 of the 65 states on which
all production call sites are vacuous.  It is sound but too conservative to infer a generally
optimal arming depth from length and mass alone.

The 16 retained exhaustive multipart states in `data/exhaustive_multipart.csv` give a less synthetic
control.  Four have no potentially useful production suffix, ten have no nonempty hereditary
pliable tail, and two have partial tails.  The direct theorem proves the complete no-call result for
two of the four.  In particular:

- the historical hard eight-part positive has `C=81`, `M=229`, `sigma=14`; exactly its final
  one-part suffix is hereditary pliable, while five callable suffixes remain potentially useful;
- the information-tight fourteen-part deadline state has `C=81`, `M=243`, `sigma=0`; its hereditary
  tail is empty and eight callable suffixes are potentially useful;
- the exact negative `Sb(111:3,115:2,121:1)@7` has `C=729`, `M=684`, `sigma=1503>=2C`; every suffix
  is pliable, so joint reachability cannot refute it after any prefix.  This matches the earlier
  observation that its root is ALIVE despite the full solver refutation.

## Decision

Keep the production cost-based trigger.  Absolute slack is the right invariant in the proofs and is
more informative than a saturation fraction, but neither slack nor nonunit length alone determines
pliability.  The cheap theorem proves some complete no-op cases, yet misses more than half of them in
the complete small census and misses important partial cutoffs in real states.

The exact `exact_head` is useful as a diagnostic and could safely suppress `rb_dead` calls at or
after the certified tail once the DP has already been built.  That is a narrower future performance
experiment, not an a-priori reason to build the DP eagerly: it pays the existing DP first and needs
per-depth call counts to show that the avoided O(1) lookups matter.
