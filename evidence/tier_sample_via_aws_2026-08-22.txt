Systematic k7 2-winner/4-winner tier sample via the persistent oracle, 2026-08-22
Status: measured. Tool: tools/ml/tier_sample_via_aws.py (batch generalization of
real_benchmark_via_aws.py, adding a mandatory natural-order control per endpoint).
Follows evidence/real_benchmark_via_aws_oracle_2026-08-22.txt's single-endpoint result; this
is the systematic sample across the corpus's hardest 2-winner and 4-winner tiers that note left
pending.

=====================================================================================
1. A REAL BUG, CAUGHT BEFORE TRUSTING ANY TIER-LEVEL NUMBER: SUBSAMPLING CAN ERASE ALL
   KNOWN WINNERS FROM THE POOL BEING RANKED
=====================================================================================
First version of the script reused real_benchmark_via_aws.py's cand_cap=20000 pattern: build
the exact stage-2 candidate set, then randomly subsample it down to 20,000 if larger, THEN rank
and walk. A first 2-endpoint pilot (1 endpoint per tier) gave:

  2-winner U000368 (Sb(14:5,9:6,13:4,9:5)@5): rank_learned=1, rank_natural=None (hit the 8,000
    natural-order cap without success)
  4-winner U000559 (Sb(14:6,15:3,9:5,9:5)@5): rank_learned=None, rank_natural=None -- BOTH
    orders found nothing

The second result looked like a null result about the ordering. It was not. Checking offline
(no oracle calls, pure census-data lookup):

  true stage-2 size (unsampled): U000368 = 56,798,  U000559 = 60,534
  literal census winners present in the FULL stage-2 set: 2/2 and 4/4 respectively

Both endpoints' known winners are legitimate, R_0-admissible members of the true candidate
space -- exactly as they must be, since they are certified solving splits. The 20,000-candidate
subsample (35% and 33% retention respectively) simply failed to include ANY of the 2-4 known
winners for U000559 by chance: with a ~65-67% per-winner exclusion probability at that retention
rate, P(all N winners excluded) is not small for N=2..4 (roughly 20% for N=4). Since
`rank_learned` scans EVERY R_0-survivor of the sampled pool exhaustively (2,341 of them, for
U000559's sample) and still found nothing, the winners were almost certainly not in the sampled
20,000 at all -- confirmed by the true-pool membership check above. This is exactly the
missing-from-stage2 risk that tools/ml/census_sample_via_aws_oracle.py's docstring already
flagged for a different reason (enumerate's own re-sorting); here it is the SAME risk from plain
random subsampling, applied specifically to the rare-winner (hard) population this whole track
exists to test -- so it is not a corner case, it is close to the modal case for this population.

FIX (in tools/ml/tier_sample_via_aws.py): raise the default cand_cap from 20,000 to 150,000 --
well above this corpus's typical true stage-2 size (~50-60k for a k7-census 4-part forced
endpoint) -- so ordinary endpoints are ranked over their FULL true candidate pool, never a
subsample. A truncation still triggers (and is now logged with an explicit WARNING line) if a
genuine outlier exceeds 150,000; per-endpoint output also now reports "known winners in pool:
X/Y" so a future silent gap of this kind cannot hide in a rank number again. real_benchmark_via_
aws.py has the same 20,000-cap pattern (used successfully so far because both its measured
endpoints happened not to be hit by this) -- it should get the same fix before its next use on a
new endpoint; not yet done as of this note.

=====================================================================================
2. THE RE-RUN, WITH THE FIX: BOTH ENDPOINTS NOW RESOLVE
=====================================================================================
Same two endpoints, same corpus/tiers, cand_cap=150000 (i.e. unsampled for both -- true sizes
56,798 and 60,534 are under the cap):

  2-winner U000368 (Sb(14:5,9:6,13:4,9:5)@5), mass 221/243:
    stage-2 = 56,798  (2/2 known winners present, as expected once unsampled)
    R_0 survivors = 7,666 (13.5%)
    rank_learned = 1      rank_natural = 6,041      SAME split, both orders   [394s]
    -> selectivity 6,041x

  4-winner U000559 (Sb(14:6,15:3,9:5,9:5)@5), mass 219/243:
    stage-2 = 60,534  (4/4 known winners present)
    R_0 survivors = 7,152 (11.8%)
    rank_learned = 13     rank_natural = 4,272      SAME split, both orders   [310s]
    -> selectivity 328.6x

Both are real, complete (non-capped) measurements: neither order hit its try-cap, so these are
exact ranks, not lower bounds. Total wall time for both endpoints: 705s (~5.9 min/endpoint
average), all against the persistent oracle over the SSM tunnel.

Note the 4-winner endpoint's much smaller selectivity (328.6x vs 6,041x) despite having MORE
known winners, not fewer -- a reminder that winner *count* alone doesn't predict how favorably
the learned order does; rank_learned=13 here (not 1) means even the recursive-V order took a
handful of tries on this state, worth keeping in mind before assuming "more winners = easier for
everyone" or "learned order always lands at #1."

=====================================================================================
3. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH
=====================================================================================
* n=2 endpoints (1 per tier), both now with COMPLETE (uncapped) natural-order ranks, on top of
  the n=1 partial result in real_benchmark_via_aws_oracle_2026-08-22.txt (same U000368 endpoint,
  consistent: rank_learned=1 there too; that note's rank_natural=2,970 came from a different
  random subsample/order than this one's 6,041 -- both are real measurements of the same
  underlying fact ("natural order needs thousands of tries here"), not a contradiction; the
  exact natural rank depends on which specific enumeration order is walked, only the ORDER OF
  MAGNITUDE (thousands, not tens) is the load-bearing claim).
* Both endpoints were chosen as the HIGHEST-mass (hardest) in their tier, per the trivial-state-
  trap lesson -- not a random or convenient pick.
* A full n_per_tier=8 systematic sample (16 endpoints, 8 per tier) was launched after this fix
  to get median/spread statistics across the tiers rather than n=1 each; see the summary
  appended below once it completes, or docs/journal.md's dated entry if this file was not
  updated afterward.
* Per-endpoint cost with the fix (~5-6 min/endpoint, dominated by the natural-order control's
  ~60-130ms/candidate oracle round-trip) means a 16-endpoint sample costs on the order of 90
  minutes of wall clock against the shared persistent oracle -- a real, non-trivial cost, run
  with the user's explicit go-ahead given the repo's ask-before-long-runs convention.

Reproduce: `tools/ml/tier_sample_via_aws.py k7 1 150000 8000` (single endpoint per tier) or
`tools/ml/tier_sample_via_aws.py k7 8 150000 8000` (full sample), with an SSM tunnel open to the
oracle-serve instance on 127.0.0.1:7777 (see docs/aws-run.md).

=====================================================================================
4. THE FULL 16-ENDPOINT SAMPLE (8 per tier), RUN AFTER THE FIX
=====================================================================================
9,985s (2h 46m) total, ~624s/endpoint average, all against the persistent oracle over the SSM
tunnel (which needed one mid-run reconnect -- see the trap note above; the process itself did
not crash, a separate diagnostic connection did, before the batch was relaunched).

RANK_LEARNED is real and complete for all 16 endpoints -- it is bounded only by the R_0-survivor
count (typically several thousand, far under the 150,000 cap), so it never hit a try-cap:

  2-winner (n=8): rank_learned = 1, 1, 5, 5, 9, 13, 75, 104        median 7,    max 104
  4-winner (n=8): rank_learned = 15, 43, 79, 81, 85, 123, 191, 687  median 83,  max 687

RANK_NATURAL is the weak link in this particular run: the 8,000-try cap (chosen for the earlier
2-endpoint pilot's timing, not re-examined before the full run) resolved only 3/8 2-winner and
1/8 4-winner endpoints. Read the natural_cap number itself as an artifact of this run's time
budget, not a property of the states.

  RESOLVED (natural rank measured exactly, both orders succeeded within cap):
    2-winner: 3/8 -- selectivity 559.6x, 4,691x, 6,041x  (median 4,691x)
    4-winner: 1/8 -- selectivity 68.9x

  UNRESOLVED (natural order exhausted 8,000 tries without success -- true rank is >8,000, so
  these are LOWER bounds on selectivity, computed as 8000/rank_learned):
    2-winner (5/8): >=615x, >=77x, >=1,600x, >=107x, >=1,600x
    4-winner (7/8): >=42x, >=186x, >=12x, >=99x, >=101x, >=65x, >=533x

Do not average or median the *known* selectivity numbers alone and call it "the" tier median --
that silently drops the harder-to-resolve half to two-thirds of each tier, and there is no reason
to believe the excluded endpoints are systematically easier OR harder than the resolved ones (the
weakest lower bound in the whole sample, 4-winner U000068's >=11.6x, sits BELOW several of the
fully-resolved values, so exclusion bias does not obviously run in one direction here). The
honest summary is two separate, complete facts: (a) the learned order's own rank is small and
fully measured for every endpoint (max 104 of up to ~75,000 candidates in the 2-winner tier, max
687 in the 4-winner tier), and (b) the natural order's rank, wherever it could be measured at all,
was in the thousands -- with the remaining majority of endpoints only bounded below, not
resolved.

One endpoint stands out: 4-winner U000068 (mass 165/243, 68% fill -- notably lower than every
other sampled endpoint's ~87-91%) has the sample's worst rank_learned (687) and weakest lower
bound (>=11.6x). This is not evidence the method fails on lower-mass states in general (n=1); it
is a flag that the "hardest-endpoint" sampling bias did NOT actually apply to the 4-winner tier --
see below.

TIER-4 SAMPLING CAVEAT: `hardest()` takes the top `3*n_per_tier` candidates by mass, then samples
n_per_tier from that pool. The 2-winner tier has 131 endpoints, so top-24-of-131 is a genuine
hardest-mass bias (all 8 sampled 2-winner endpoints landed at 218-221 of 243, i.e. 89.7-90.9%
fill). The 4-winner tier has only 22 endpoints total, so "top 24" is the entire tier -- the
4-winner sample is an unbiased draw across ALL 22 endpoints, not a hardest-biased one, which is
exactly why its masses range from 68% to 92% instead of clustering near the cap like the 2-winner
tier's. Not a bug -- both tiers were sampled by the same code -- but the two tiers' results are
not directly comparable as "hardest vs hardest"; the 4-winner numbers describe this tier's full
population, the 2-winner numbers describe specifically its hardest octile.

SPLIT AGREEMENT: of the 4 endpoints where both orders resolved, only 2 (U000368, U000607) found
the exact identical split; the other 2 (U000534, U000547) found different splits. Both are
equally valid: `run_order` only accepts a candidate when the real oracle certifies all three
children SOLVABLE, independent of whether that specific split matches a census-recorded literal
winner -- states in this population have more valid solving splits than the 2-4 the census
happened to enumerate, and both orders are free to land on any of them.

WHAT WOULD FIX THE CENSORING FOR A FUTURE RUN: raise natural_cap well past 8,000 (say 15-20k,
matching the observed 4,691-6,041 range with headroom) at the cost of proportionally more wall
time on the endpoints that still don't resolve, or accept the lower-bound-only outcome as the
honest result for a hard-tail population where "even a raw walk of tens of thousands rarely
finds a winner" is itself part of the finding.
