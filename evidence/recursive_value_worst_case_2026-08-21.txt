Worst case first, then order: composing a sound filter with the recursive scorer, 2026-08-21
Status: measured. Script: tools/ml/recursive_value.py (Experiment 3, `worst_case_then_order`).
Continues evidence/recursive_value_2026-08-20.txt. Prompted by two user challenges in sequence:
(1) a random-sampler-trained value model mostly proves itself on "easy" cases; the hard cases --
few winning splits -- are what actually matters and were undertested; (2) instead of chasing the
exact winning split, optimize the WORST case first (a sound early-stop point) and only then worry
about ordering for the typical case.

=====================================================================================
0. THE CHALLENGE, CONFIRMED FIRST
=====================================================================================
Re-ran experiment 2 (evidence/recursive_value_2026-08-20.txt section 2) against the EXACT stage-2
candidate enumeration (`exact_topk.py`'s method, no 6,000-candidate sampling cap) and stratified
by exact candidate-set size -- the real hardness axis here, since the literal winning-cut count
turned out to be nearly constant (2 or 4, almost always just the trivial complementation pair) and
carries no stratifying information on its own.

  recursive logistic-V, scaled to the full exact space: median rank 198, worst 16,886 (of up to
  130,262) -- essentially tied with a flat ranker trained WITH direct split supervision on this
  same population (193, worst 8,834), so the "recursion matches supervision" finding holds up on
  the harder exact protocol.

  stratified by exact stage-2 candidate-set size (thirds, easiest -> hardest):
    7,684-40,610 candidates:    median rank 177   worst  4,082   top-100 33.3%
    41,278-60,636 candidates:   median rank 146   worst 15,315   top-100 35.3%
    60,760-130,262 candidates:  median rank 331   worst 16,886   top-100 19.6%

The hardest third really is worse: median rank roughly doubles, top-100 hit rate drops by nearly
half. The user's concern was correct, on this population, before anything else was tried.

=====================================================================================
1. THE REFRAME: A RANKING CANNOT CERTIFY A NEGATIVE. A SOUND FILTER CAN.
=====================================================================================
No ordering, however good on the typical case, changes how many candidates must be exhausted to
PROVE none of them work -- that number can only be shrunk by a proven necessary condition. This
repo already has one, unused in this context: `R_0` (full-star majorization, proved in
docs/theorems/singleton-majorization.md, implemented as `tools/bundled_majorization.r0` and, for
the production solver, `radiobase.c`'s `star_expansion_majorization_can_solve`). It had been
measured before (docs/journal.md, `evidence/learned_cut_ranker_2026-08-20.txt`) but never: (a)
composed specifically on top of the stage-2 (cap + per-part-Pareto) candidate set used throughout
this ML thread, (b) stratified by hardness to check whether it holds up on the hard tier, or (c)
combined with the recursive scorer and measured as one pipeline.

**Two guarantees, kept deliberately separate, exactly per the achievability/unsolvability
asymmetry already on record:**
- `R_0`-survivor count: the real, sound, theorem-backed worst-case bound. Exhausting every
  survivor with no success is a valid proof of unsolvability. No learned model is involved.
- Rank of the winner WITHIN the survivors, by the recursive V scorer: unsound, ordering only.
  Speeds up finding a witness when one exists; must never be used to stop early or declare
  unsolvable -- the 2023 corpus's 37 false negatives are what that shortcut costs.

=====================================================================================
2. MEASURED: COMPOSING THEM FIXES BOTH PROBLEMS AT ONCE
=====================================================================================
Same 153 forced k7 endpoints, same exact stage-2 enumeration, same <=20,000-candidate uniform
subsample (winners always kept) used for tractability. `R_0` applied to each candidate's three
children (its own semantic-state convention, NOT `analyze_single_solution_cuts.children`'s --
that one drops n*m<=1 "unit" parts that `bm.r0` was not built to have pre-pruned; see
`_r0_children` in the script). Soundness spot-check: **0 winners dropped by R_0** across every
sampled candidate at every endpoint, as the theorem requires.

  stage-2 candidates: median 54,014, max 130,262
  R_0 survivors (SOUND worst-case bound), scaled to the full space:
    median 6,892, max 16,547 -- a median 6.1x shrink beyond stage-2 alone
  rank of the winner WITHIN the R_0 survivors (recursive V order):
    median 14, worst 954

That worst-case rank (954, and a median of 14) is already far better than the pre-filter numbers
(331 median / 16,886 worst on the hardest tier) -- but the more important result is what happens
under the SAME hardness stratification as section 0:

  stage-2 size tier          R_0 survivors (median/max)   shrink    rank median/worst
  7,684-40,610 (easiest)     5,924 / 11,277                4.7x       18 / 528
  41,278-60,636              8,162 / 13,369                6.1x       12 / 954
  60,760-130,262 (hardest)   7,940 / 16,547               10.6x       13 / 943

  correlation(stage-2 size, R_0-survivor count) =  0.257
  correlation(stage-2 size, rank-within-R_0)    =  0.001

**`R_0` shrinks the hardest tier MORE, not less** (10.6x vs 4.7x on the easiest) -- the opposite
of what you'd fear from "the hard cases are hard because nothing distinguishes them." And once
`R_0` has removed whatever it removes, **the recursive ranker's degradation on hard cases
disappears entirely**: rank medians are 18/12/13 across the three tiers (flat, not "roughly
double" as before `R_0`), and the correlation between hardness and rank drops from 0.129
(pre-filter, evidence/recursive_value_2026-08-20.txt section 2's own follow-up check) to 0.001.

**Reading:** the earlier degradation on the hard tier was not evidence that the recursive value
model gets confused by hard cases specifically. It was evidence that the hard tier's larger raw
candidate space contains more candidates that only a *sound* condition can rule out -- once `R_0`
removes those first, the residual population the learned ranker actually has to sort is no harder,
on average, than the easy tier's. Sound filtering and learned ordering are doing genuinely
different jobs here, and composing them is far better than either alone.

=====================================================================================
3. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT LICENSE
=====================================================================================
* "Optimize the worst case first, then order" is a sound, validated objective, and this composed
  pipeline is the first concrete instance of it in the ML thread. The `R_0`-survivor count (median
  6,892, worst 16,547) is the number to quote as the current cutoff-with-proof bound -- still in
  the thousands, not small, but a real, theorem-backed improvement over stage-2's up-to-130,262,
  and one that specifically holds up (in fact improves) on the hardest cases.
* This is NOT the full worst-case story. `R_0`'s own ladder continues (`R_1`, deeper `R_d`,
  converging to exact solving per the Synchronized Hierarchy Theorem) and the "cross-part pair"
  condition (`tools/split_choice_rules.py`, previously measured at 140.4x combined with `R_0` on a
  different, smaller denominator and NOT stratified by hardness) is the natural next sound filter
  to compose and re-test on this same hardness stratification. Neither is done here.
* The 6.1x/10.6x shrink numbers are against THIS thread's stage-2 denominator (cap + per-part
  Pareto). The previously-quoted 16.1x (`R_0` alone) and 140.4x (`R_0`+pairs) figures in
  docs/journal.md are against a different, smaller "cap + four-rectangle frontier" denominator on
  a 25-endpoint subsample -- the numbers are not directly comparable across denominators, and
  neither superseded the other; they answer different questions on different populations.
* Still untested: k=8 (children at k=7) and the real k=9 H2 frontier. k=7-rooted sub-searches are
  exactly what crashed the warm oracle building this thread's own training data (front-record
  handle exhaustion, see evidence/recursive_value_2026-08-20.txt section 0b) -- expect that to
  recur if this is extended to k=8 without a fix or a much smaller budget per query.
* Reproduce: `.venv/bin/python tools/ml/recursive_value.py` (Experiment 3 section). Needs
  `tools/bundled_majorization.py` (already committed, no third-party deps) alongside the usual
  `.venv` numpy/scikit-learn and the pulled k7/k8 census artifacts.
