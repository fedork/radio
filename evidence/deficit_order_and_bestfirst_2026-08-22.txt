Prototyping the two 2026-08-22 proposals ("improve BY_MAGIC3" / "direct generation without large
enumeration"), tested against the real persistent oracle, 2026-08-22
Status: measured. Tool: tools/ml/proto_deficit_bestfirst.py.

BACKGROUND. Both proposals were motivated by one existing offline measurement
(tools/ml/recursive_value.py's own baseline check): the per-part "Pareto deficit" -- how far the
worst rectangle in a child sits below the proven Sb-Pareto frontier at k-1, `deficit(parts,k) =
max over parts of (n - MAXN1[k][m])` -- gets AUC 0.9961 ALONE as a per-state solvability
classifier, nearly matching the full pooled recursive-V model (logistic 0.9861, gradient boosting
0.9956) and decisively beating plain mass/cap (0.5923). Two ideas followed: (1) swap this signal
in for BY_MAGIC3's current "distance from the geometric midpoint" heuristic, since it is already
computed at the exact point `split_admissible`/`split_child_admissible` run in radiobase.c, at
zero extra enumeration cost; (2) since deficit is a MAX over independent per-part terms, generate
candidates best-first via a priority queue over per-part choices, without ever building the full
R_0-survivor list.

=====================================================================================
0. A REAL BUG CAUGHT WHILE VALIDATING THE DECOMPOSITION -- BEFORE TRUSTING EITHER PROTOTYPE
=====================================================================================
The claim "deficit is a max over independent per-part terms" needed checking, not assuming.
First cross-check (whole-child deficit computed the normal way, via recursive_value.deficit() on
each of selected/mixed/complement, vs. a per-part decomposition summing the four shapes each part
feeds) gave 1833/2000 MISMATCHES on random splits of a real state. Root cause: `bm.normalize`
drops any rectangle with a zero side (x<=0 or y<=0 -- not a real constraint) before deficit() ever
sees it, but the naive combination rule `max(deficit(sel), deficit(mix), deficit(comp))` was
feeding `deficit()`'s own "-1.0 = trivially fine, empty child" SENTINEL into that same max() as if
it were a real, comparable value -- and -1.0 frequently beats a real child's genuine (much more
negative, i.e. better-margin) deficit, incorrectly making an EMPTY, unconstrained child look like
the state's worst-case bottleneck. Fixed by excluding empty children from the combination (an
empty child imposes no constraint and can never be the bottleneck) rather than passing its
sentinel through arithmetic meant for real values. Re-checked over 20,000 random splits: 0
mismatches. The per-part decomposition is genuinely correct once this is fixed; recorded here
because it is exactly the kind of quiet, plausible-looking error the repo's "verify before
relying" discipline exists to catch, and because it briefly made prototype 2 look far more broken
than it already was on its own.

=====================================================================================
1. PROTOTYPE 1 -- DEFICIT ORDER, TESTED ON ALL 4 CHARACTERIZED ENDPOINTS FROM THE TIER SAMPLE
=====================================================================================
Same 4 real k7-census endpoints as evidence/tier_sample_via_aws_2026-08-22.txt (parts looked up
live from CORPORA["k7"], never hand-typed -- see section 4). R_0-survivor counts reproduce that
run's numbers exactly (a live cross-check that these are genuinely the same states). Candidates
ordered by ascending `deficit` (lower deficit = more margin = tried first) instead of the pooled
recursive-V score, walked against the real oracle:

  endpoint   R_0 survivors   rank_learned (pooled)   rank_DEFICIT   rank_natural (blind)
  U000368    7,666           1                        2,698          6,041
  U000535    6,992           13                       2,283          >8,000 (capped)
  U000607    10,940          85                       1,260          5,860
  U000068    3,436           687                      2,916          >8,000 (capped)

Deficit order is a real, complete, working order in every case -- it always resolves (never hit
a try-cap), and it beats blind/natural order by a wide margin (2.2x-4.7x where natural itself
resolved; a real, finite rank where natural didn't resolve at all within its budget). But it is
consistently, substantially worse than the pooled recursive-V model -- 1,000x-2,700x worse on the
easy end (U000368: 1 vs 2,698), though notably the GAP narrows on the hardest case (U000068: 687
vs 2,916, only ~4.2x) -- a pattern worth more data before reading anything into it (n=4).

=====================================================================================
2. WHY DEFICIT UNDERPERFORMS: IT SATURATES WITHIN THE R_0-SURVIVOR POPULATION
=====================================================================================
Checked the actual deficit distribution across ALL stage-2 (mass-feasible) candidates, not just
R_0-survivors, for all 4 endpoints:

  endpoint    stage-2 size   deficit range   candidates AT the worst value (0)
  U000368     56,798         [-2, 0]         47,128 (83.0%)
  U000535     41,912         [-2, 0]         34,444 (82.2%)
  U000607     42,338         [-2, 0]         39,508 (93.3%)
  U000068     85,286         [-2, 0]         62,374 (73.1%)

73-93% of EVERY tested endpoint's mass-feasible candidates already sit at deficit=0 -- the single
WORST value a candidate can have and still be admissible at all. This is a real property of these
states, not noise: they are near-cap-mass (87-93% of the parent's information cap), so hitting the
EXACT total mass required routinely forces at least one part to sit right at its own individual
boundary, regardless of how comfortably the other parts fit. The offline AUC (0.9961) was measured
ACROSS a population of states spanning easy to hard; deficit correctly separates that population
(most states are nowhere near their cap and get comfortably negative deficits). But CONDITIONED on
already being in the R_0-survivor set of one specific hard, near-cap-mass endpoint -- which is
exactly the population an ordering signal has to discriminate within -- deficit has almost no
dynamic range left. This is the same shape as Berkson's-paradox-style range restriction: a strong
population-level predictor can carry near-zero information once you condition on the population
already having passed through the filter it correlates with.

A same-cost variant tried before writing this up: SUM the per-part slack across every rectangle
in every child, instead of taking the MAX. This has much better dynamic range (94 distinct values
among U000368's 7,666 survivors, vs 3 for max-deficit) and modestly improves the rank of both
known literal winners (2,196 and 2,218, vs max-deficit's 2,698) -- but is still nowhere near the
pooled model's rank 1. More granularity alone doesn't close the gap; whatever the pooled model
captures beyond per-part slack (likely some cross-part balance/interaction signal, given it pools
mean/std/aspect statistics across all parts together) isn't recovered by summing instead of
maxing the same underlying per-part number.

=====================================================================================
3. PROTOTYPE 2 -- NAIVE BEST-FIRST GENERATION: FAILS, DIAGNOSED PRECISELY
=====================================================================================
Implemented a heap-based best-first generator: each part's own admissible options pre-sorted by
that part's own worst-shape deficit; states are index-tuples across the 4 parts; pop the
lowest-max-deficit tuple, check joint mass/cap feasibility, push single-index-increment neighbors.
Tested exhaustively on U000368 (the easiest of the 4 for every other method): 200,000 pops,
**zero** mass-feasible candidates found (let alone a solvable one).

Root cause, confirmed directly: both of U000368's known literal winners require, in at least one
part, an option ranked 36th-of-46 or 59th-of-60 within that PART's own deficit-sorted list --
i.e. deep in the "worse" end of that single part's range, not near its own best choice. The
threshold needed to admit either winner is deficit=0 in EVERY part simultaneously (confirmed via
direct computation) -- which, per section 2, is also the single worst value across the board. A
frontier search over 4 independent dimensions needs to explore roughly the PRODUCT of each
dimension's per-threshold option count before reaching a given threshold, not the sum: at
threshold 0, that product is the full per-part cross product, 46*62*54*60 = 9,240,480. This is a
genuine, diagnosed algorithmic limitation of independent-dimension best-first search once the
target sits near the boundary in every dimension at once -- not a coding bug, and not specific to
this one endpoint (the section-2 distribution shows every tested endpoint has the same
73-93%-at-the-boundary shape, so the same failure mode is expected to generalize, and was not
re-run to exhaustion on the other 3 endpoints for that reason -- an explicit, deliberate scope
limit, not an oversight: repeating a diagnosed, expected-negative 200,000-pop run three more times
would spend real oracle-adjacent time for no new information).

=====================================================================================
4. SCOPE CHECK: DOES "AVOID FULL ENUMERATION" EVEN MATTER AT THE SCALE TESTED?
=====================================================================================
Before concluding, checked whether full materialization is actually expensive at the scale this
thread has validated on (k7, 4 parts, stage-2 tens of thousands): it is not. `exact_candidates`'s
vectorized numpy DP builds the full (mass-feasible) stage-2 set in well under a second; R_0
filtering plus pooled feature computation over the full stage-2 set completes in single-digit
seconds (measured throughout this session's other work). "Avoid enumeration" is not a live
constraint at this scale.

It IS a live constraint one level up: CORPORA["k8"]'s hardest 4-part endpoints (mass ~88% of a
larger cap, since rk=6 there) have stage-2 sizes of 3.3-3.9 MILLION candidates (measured on the
top 3 highest-mass k8 endpoints: 3,866,716 / 3,429,504 / 3,282,604). At that scale, the CURRENT
(unvectorized, per-candidate Python loop) R_0-plus-feature-computation pipeline extrapolates to
about 3.2 minutes for one endpoint (measured on a 3,000-candidate sample of U000054: 0.050
ms/candidate including R_0, ~25% R_0 survival rate, ~967,000 estimated survivors). Real, but not
extreme -- and notably, the naive best-first generator would very likely hit the exact same
saturation failure at this scale too, since section 2's finding (near-cap-mass forces some part to
its own boundary) is a property of high mass-fill states in general, not of k7's particular sizes.
So scaling up would not rescue prototype 2 as designed; the SCORE, not the search algorithm, is
the limiting factor. The more promising fix for the k8-scale cost, if it is ever needed, is
vectorizing the existing per-candidate R_0/feature loop the same way stage-2 candidate generation
already is -- an engineering change, not a new algorithm -- not yet done or tested.

=====================================================================================
5. WHAT THIS ESTABLISHES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
=====================================================================================
* BY_MAGIC3 CAN be improved cheaply and safely: deficit order is a real, complete, working
  improvement over blind/natural order (2.2x-4.7x where comparable, and resolves several cases
  natural order couldn't resolve at all within budget) at zero additional enumeration cost, since
  the underlying signal is already computed at split-table-build time in radiobase.c. It is NOT a
  substitute for the full pooled model where that is affordable -- it is a candidate for the
  cheap, always-on static-heuristic tier only.
* Direct generation via a deficit-driven best-first search, AS SPECIFIED, does not work, for a
  clearly diagnosed structural reason (near-cap-mass states force per-part deficit to saturate at
  its worst admissible value for the large majority of feasible candidates, including known
  winners) -- not a scale problem, not a bug, a genuine mismatch between what this particular
  signal measures and what the search needs to prioritize. This is recorded as a negative result
  specifically so the same mechanism is not re-attempted from scratch later.
* A more sophisticated DP/beam-integrated best-first search (combining the existing (S,X)
  mass-feasibility DP with a running best-path priority, Dijkstra/bottleneck-shortest-path style)
  was designed but NOT built or tested -- deliberately, because it would very likely inherit the
  same saturation problem from the same underlying score, making it low-expected-value to build
  before a better decomposable score exists. Flagged as the natural next step only if paired with
  a genuinely different, non-saturating decomposable signal.
* n=4 endpoints, all from the same k7 corpus and the same near-cap-mass, few-known-winner
  population this whole thread has focused on. Generalization beyond that population (e.g. lower
  mass-fill states, where deficit would plausibly have much better dynamic range within R_0
  survivors too) is untested.

Reproduce: `tools/ml/proto_deficit_bestfirst.py [endpoint_name ...]` (defaults to all 4), with an
SSM tunnel open to the oracle-serve instance on 127.0.0.1:7777 (see docs/aws-run.md).

=====================================================================================
6. FOLLOW-UP: BLOCK COORDINATE DESCENT (a real packing-problem framing), tested for real
=====================================================================================
Prompted by naming the problem precisely: the mass/cap constraint is a multiple-choice knapsack,
already solved exactly by `exact_candidates`'s (S,X) DP -- the open problem is that the actual
quality signal (does a combination lead to three solvable children) is a genuinely joint,
non-separable function of the parts, which no per-part-decomposable proxy (deficit, sum-of-slack)
can capture. Coordinate descent sidesteps this by using the EXPENSIVE, ACCURATE pooled
recursive-V score directly, evaluated only on a small free block of parts at a time, holding the
rest fixed. Tool: tools/ml/proto_coord_descent.py.

1-PART-AT-A-TIME DESCENT FAILS OUTRIGHT: tested with real oracle verification on U000368 (the
easiest of the 4 endpoints for every other method) -- 30 restarts, 10 rounds each, 1,765 total
pooled-score evaluations, ZERO real successes. Offline (no oracle, comparing against the known
winner's own score) confirms why: even 300 restarts (19,392 evaluations) plateau at score 0.039,
never reaching the known winner's own score of 0.059 under the same model instance -- the
AND-of-three-children landscape is rugged enough that single-part moves cannot climb out of the
local optima a random start lands in.

2-PARTS-AT-A-TIME (BLOCK) DESCENT WORKS ON THE EASIER HALF, FAILS DECISIVELY ON THE HARDER HALF:
jointly re-optimizing one PAIR of parts at a time (holding the other two fixed, searching that
pair's own admissible-option cross product, still small -- up to ~60x60) gives real success:

  endpoint   R_0 survivors  pooled rank_learned  2-block descent result
  U000368    7,666          1                    SUCCESS, 18,447 evals (2.4x R_0 count)
  U000535    6,992          13                   SUCCESS, 7,046 evals (1.0x R_0 count)
  U000607    10,940         85                   FAILED after 150 restarts, 257,591 evals (23.5x)
  U000068    3,436          687                  FAILED after 150 restarts, 774,712 evals (225x)

Both successes are real, oracle-verified splits -- U000368's matches a known census literal winner
exactly; U000535's ((5,2),(5,3),(9,5),(4,0)) does not match either known census winner, confirming
(again) that these hard states have valid solving splits beyond the 2-4 the census happened to
record. But both failures are decisive, not just "needs a bit more budget": 150 restarts (up to
774,712 evaluations, ~225x the endpoint's own R_0-survivor count) never found a working
combination on the two endpoints where the pooled model itself needed the MOST tries (rank 85 and
687 respectively) to find a winner by directly scoring the full survivor list.

The pattern lines up with what pooled rank_learned already told us: 2-block descent succeeds
exactly on the endpoints that were already "easy" for the pooled model (rank 1, 13) and fails on
the ones that were already "hard" for it (rank 85, 687) -- consistent with a genuinely narrower,
harder-to-find optimum on those states that local search, even with a larger neighborhood and 150
restarts, does not reliably locate, while directly scoring the (still modestly sized, 3.4k-11k)
full survivor list does.

COST COMPARISON, HONESTLY: at the k7 scale tested, 2-block descent is NOT cheaper than simply
scoring the full R_0-survivor list directly with the pooled model, even on the two endpoints where
it succeeds (2.4x and 1.0x the direct-scan cost, not a fraction of it) -- let alone the two where
it fails at 23-225x the cost with nothing to show for it. Its value, if any, is specifically for
scales where the direct scan itself becomes the bottleneck (the k8 3-9M-candidate endpoints
measured in section 4) -- untested here, and given the failure pattern above, there is no
particular reason to expect coordinate descent to do better on hard cases at that scale either;
if anything the harder-endpoint failures here suggest the opposite.

WHAT THIS ESTABLISHES: block coordinate descent is a real, working algorithm -- unlike deficit
best-first, it does find genuine winners, some of which are not census-recorded, using the
already-validated pooled score rather than a separable proxy. But it is not a general solution:
it works on the population's easier half and fails, expensively and decisively, on the harder
half -- precisely the population (few known winners, deep in the search space) this whole track
exists to help with. Not recommended for adoption over direct full-list scoring at the scale
tested; worth reconsidering only if a genuinely different escape mechanism (simulated annealing,
3+-block moves, or a smarter non-uniform restart distribution) is tried and shown to close the gap
on the hard half specifically -- none of which has been attempted.

Reproduce: `tools/ml/proto_coord_descent.py [endpoint_name ...]` (defaults to all 4), with an SSM
tunnel open to the oracle-serve instance on 127.0.0.1:7777 (see docs/aws-run.md).
