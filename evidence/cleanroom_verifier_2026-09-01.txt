Cleanroom verification of the Sa(193) certificate of record
============================================================
Record of note: the complete-chain run 20260902T045843Z (2026-09-01, finished 23:50 PDT).
An earlier k=7-only run, 20260902T010521Z, is retained below as the first result and as the

The certificate of record (artifact sa193-certificate-2026-08-19, 8 level-v2 files,
2,846,568 claims) is verified end to end by an implementation that shares no code with the
solver: tools/cleanroom, Rust, standard library only, every rule recomputed from the
statement it cites.  This is the first independent semantic verification of the chain.
Before it, `tools/check_level_chain.py` checked structure only, and every semantic replay was
`radio_refute.c`, which `#include`s `radiobase.c` and is therefore solver-core validation
rather than a second implementation.

Trust base and what it rests on
-------------------------------
Inputs the checker was written from: docs/problem.md, docs/theorems/*.md, and the certificate
grammars in docs/certificate.md.  It reads no cache, includes no solver source, and recomputes
G_k from its defining recurrence (the closed form appears only as a test).  The rules are
exactly INFO, STAR (tail-clamped), DOM, and UNIT in canonicalization, applied to full or
partial children under Subgraph Monotonicity.  Split-space quotients are SYM-E (equal-part,
non-decreasing option index), SYM-S (square-part shore swap, an option-domain restriction),
and SYM-C (one whole-vector complement rule); the composition argument is in audit.rs and the
regression for it is the exhaustive selftest below.

Agreement with the production engine, and cost
----------------------------------------------
Same search tree at full scale.  On k=7 the cleanroom checker charged 3,234,992,515,839
candidate cells against the frozen refuter's 3,220,215,775,519 accepted prefixes - 0.46% apart
over 3.2 trillion.  (A stride-500 sample agreed to 0.26% per part-count, with np=2 identical
at 9,858.)  No prune is missing.

Against the refuter on the same trimmed chain:
  cleanroom  191,800 CPU s /  6,575 wall s on 32 vCPU  (3.05 GB peak)
  refuter    201,983 CPU s / 12,627 wall s on 16 vCPU  (1.24 GB peak)
So 0.95x the CPU and 1.92x faster in wall clock.  The checker uses more memory, which is the
price of its pre-expanded dominance index.

Two optimizations got it there, neither touching a rule; the search tree is bit-identical
before and after (Sa(113) 1,221,827,462 cells and the k=7 sample's 6,545,617,897, unchanged to
the digit):
  * Incremental partial children.  Each cell used to rebuild every partial child from (n,m) -
    subtract, orient, strip units, insertion-sort - three times over, O(depth^2) per cell.
    Options now carry precomputed canonical pieces and universe ids, and each depth caches its
    prefix child in the two orders the rules consume (ascending id for DOM, descending width
    for STAR), so a cell merges in one or two pieces.
  * Antichain reduction of the support.  Facts are inserted in ascending (mass, parts) order
    and one whose dominator is already present is dropped with its whole closure - Subgraph
    Monotonicity corollary 3, "certificates may store antichains", at build time.
Effect: Sa(113) 135.6 -> 94.1 CPU s; k=7 sample verify 678 -> 401 CPU s; k=7 index build
366 -> 227 s with 41.1M -> 33.2M closure tuples.

Falsification evidence
----------------------
Verifying good certificates proves little on its own; a checker that accepts everything passes
that test.  What was also checked:
  * The gap fixture (a deliberately false `fact 1 Sb(1:1)`) fails closed, on the EC2 host as a
    launch gate.
  * The certificate of record's level 5 with a solvable `Sb(2:1)` claim injected: the cleanroom
    checker reports GAP verdict=uncovered and exits 1, while still verifying the 80,634 genuine
    claims.  The frozen refuter independently rejects the same file (verdict=contradicted - it
    has the positive embedded-G_k terminal, which the cleanroom checker deliberately lacks) and
    also verifies the same 80,634.
  * tools/test_cleanroom.sh, 19 checks in ~30 s: unit tests, the exhaustive selftest, both
    shared fixtures, semantic mutations against a load-bearing level (dropping level 3's
    support leaves 88 of 127 claims unverifiable), v2 grammar strictness, and thread- and
    shard-invariance of the verdict.
  * The selftest is the regression that guards the quotients: for every state with at most
    three parts, n <= 6 and k <= 4, a deliberately unquotiented naive solver written from
    docs/problem.md decides solvability, and the audit engine must verify exactly the
    unsolvable ones and refuse the solvable ones even given complete support.  That is the
    control shape that caught the 2026-08-31 quotient-composition trap.

Cost of the whole exercise: two aborted EC2 launches (~$0.85, both pipeline bugs - a
cloud-init HOME fault and a 3-hour job with no progress output), the k=7 run (~$3.3), the
complete-chain run (~$2.6), and about an hour of local CPU.

The colored-bundle gap is spurious: a citation the refuter did not make
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Followed up with the trace build (`-DRADIO_FROZEN_REFUTE_TRACE`). On the colored bundle the
refuter reports two k=5 gaps, the documented one among them:

  FROZEN_REFUTE_GAP root=Sb(15:8,8:5,8:5)[200,49] k=5
    split=Sb(7:3,8:5,3:2) Sb(7:5,8:3,0:0,0:0,5:2,3:3) Sb(8:5,0:0,5:3)
    child_verdicts=2,2,2          (2 = MAYBE: none of the three discharged)

That is the split with take vector [(8,5),(0,0),(5,3)].  Its three children at k=4, canonical:

  outcome 0  Sb(7:3,8:5,3:2)        mass 67
  outcome 1  Sb(8:3,7:5,5:2,3:3)    mass 78
  outcome 2  Sb(8:5,5:3)            mass 55

All are within the k=4 information bound of 81, and STAR refutes none of them (checked by
hand against G_4 prefix sums 16,31,42,53,58,63,68,73,74,...: the tightest is outcome 1, whose
star prefix reaches 78 against 78 at t=13 - equal, not exceeding).  So a citation is required,
and one is available:

  colored.cert line 70609:  fact 4 Sb(8:3,7:5,3:3)

`Sb(8:3,7:5,3:3)` is a literal SUB-MULTISET of the outcome-1 child `Sb(8:3,7:5,5:2,3:3)` - no
componentwise growth needed, the three parts appear exactly and the child carries one extra
part `5:2`.  By Subgraph Monotonicity corollary 1 (deleting parts yields a subgraph) the child
is unsolvable in 4, so the split IS covered and the refuter's `child_verdicts=2,2,2` is wrong
on its middle entry.  The same fact is present in the complete normalized corpus, which the
refuter closes.

Conclusion, stated at the strength the evidence supports: **this particular gap is spurious,
and the trap's premise - that the colored subset lacks needed support - is wrong for it.**
The suspicion therefore belongs with the refuter's citation lookup, not with `radio_verify.c`,
and my earlier guess that `radio_verify.c`'s strict equal-part quotient explained the
discrepancy is superseded: over-refutation there would produce false closure, but here the
closure is correct and it is the refuter that misses a real citation.

NOT established: the mechanism inside `radiobase.c`.  The fact is present in both inputs, so
presence is not the variable; something about the cache state under the thinned support set
is.  Isolating it needs instrumentation of the dominance trie, which is work on the other
verifier and was not attempted here.  A minimal one-fact certificate does not isolate it,
because the refuter returns at its FIRST uncovered split and with one support fact that is a
different split.  The remaining eight gaps were not examined individually.

Mechanism found: the production cache's lookup is positional
------------------------------------------------------------
`tools/cache_citation_probe.c` (new) asks the production dominance cache one question in
isolation: load these negative facts at level k, then is this state refuted at level k?  It
loads exactly as `load_negative_cache` does and issues the single `CACHE_ONLY` query the audit
would have issued.  The refuter's own audit path cannot be asked this - it bypasses the
audited root's cache entry and returns at its FIRST uncovered split.

Minimal reproducer, k=4:

  fact Sb(8:3,7:5,3:3)  query Sb(8:3,7:5,5:2,3:3)   -> MAYBE   (wrong: literal sub-multiset)
  fact Sb(8:3,7:5,3:3)  query Sb(8:3,7:5,3:3)       -> FALSE   (control)
  fact Sb(8:3,7:5,3:3)  query Sb(8:3,7:5,3:3,2:1)   -> FALSE   (control, extra part 2:1)

The discriminator is position, not content: adding `2:1` leaves the fact's parts a prefix of
the sorted query, adding `5:2` forces the match to skip an interior part.  Sharper, with
query `Sb(7:5,8:3,3:3)` and one-part facts:

  fact Sb(7:5) -> FALSE      fact Sb(3:3) -> FALSE      fact Sb(8:3) -> MAYBE

`Sb(8:3)` is literally one of the query's parts and is not found, while `Sb(3:3)` is found -
because `3:3 <= 7:5` componentwise and so matches the query's FIRST part.

`checkCacheTrie_ctx` (radiobase.c:1330) explains it.  It walks the query's parts in order,
using query part `i` both as the dominance test at trie level `i` (`front_find_lesser_equal`
against that node's negative front) and as the index that selects the next node
(`node = b->slot[sbb]`).  There is no search over which query part a stored part maps to, so
whether a fact is found depends on its parts aligning with the query's positions.  Complete-
ness then rests entirely on the insert-side closure having stored the right position patterns.

Consequences, stated carefully:
  * This is a COMPLETENESS defect, not a soundness one.  A missed citation makes a child look
    unrefuted, which costs search or yields MAYBE; it cannot manufacture a negative.  The
    run9 proof and the certificate of record are unaffected.
  * Its visible effect is exactly the spurious refuter gaps on the colored Sa(113) bundle.
  * The cleanroom checker is unaffected because its walk tries every distinct query position
    (dom.rs `walk`), which is a genuine sub-multiset search rather than a positional one.
  * Not established: how much of the intended coverage the insert-side closure normally
    restores, and therefore how often this bites in ordinary solving.  That wants an owner
    who knows `cacheCantSolve`'s expansion; `tools/cache_upward_closure_regression.c` is the
    existing lock on those semantics and is the place to add these cases.
