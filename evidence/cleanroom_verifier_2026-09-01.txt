Cleanroom verification of the Sa(193) certificate of record, 2026-09-01
========================================================================

The certificate of record (artifact sa193-certificate-2026-08-19, 8 level-v2 files,
2,846,568 claims) is now verified end to end by an implementation that shares no code with
the solver: tools/cleanroom, Rust, standard library only, every rule recomputed from the
statement it cites.  This is the first independent semantic verification of the chain.
Before it, `tools/check_level_chain.py` checked structure only, and every semantic replay
was `radio_refute.c`, which `#include`s `radiobase.c` and is therefore solver-core
validation rather than a second implementation.

Trust base and what it rests on
-------------------------------
Inputs the checker was written from: docs/problem.md, docs/theorems/*.md, and the
certificate grammars in docs/certificate.md.  It reads no cache, includes no solver source,
and recomputes G_k from its defining recurrence (the closed form appears only as a test).
The rules are exactly INFO, STAR (tail-clamped), DOM, and UNIT in canonicalization, applied
to full or partial children under Subgraph Monotonicity.  Split-space quotients are SYM-E
(equal-part, non-decreasing option index), SYM-S (square-part shore swap, an option-domain
restriction), and SYM-C (one whole-vector complement rule); the composition argument is in
audit.rs and the regression for it is the exhaustive selftest below.

Agreement with the production engine
------------------------------------
Same search tree, at full scale.  On k=7 the cleanroom checker charged 3,234,992,515,839
candidate cells against the frozen refuter's 3,220,215,775,519 accepted prefixes - a 0.46%
difference over 3.2 trillion.  (The stride-500 sample agreed to 0.26% per part-count, with
np=2 identical at 9,858.)  No prune is missing and none is unsound in a way that would show
as a smaller tree.

Cost, against the refuter on the same level:
  cleanroom  262,192 CPU s / 8,483 wall s on 32 vCPU
  refuter    200,939 CPU s / 12,561 wall s on 16 vCPU
So 1.30x the CPU, and 1.48x faster in wall clock on the hardware each actually used.  The
gap is constant factors, not extra work - see docs/tools.md for the profile attributing it
to child reconstruction, and the 140x index-build difference (pre-expanded up-closure tuples
versus the engine's query-time Pareto fronts).

Falsification evidence
----------------------
Verifying good certificates proves little on its own; a checker that accepts everything
passes that test.  What was also checked:
  * The gap fixture (a deliberately false `fact 1 Sb(1:1)`) fails closed, on the EC2 host as
    a launch gate.
  * The certificate of record's level 5 with a solvable `Sb(2:1)` claim injected: the
    cleanroom checker reports GAP verdict=uncovered and exits 1, while still verifying the
    80,634 genuine claims.  The frozen refuter independently rejects the same file
    (verdict=contradicted - it has the positive embedded-G_k terminal, which the cleanroom
    checker deliberately lacks) and also verifies the same 80,634.
  * tools/test_cleanroom.sh, 19 checks in ~30 s: unit tests, the exhaustive selftest, both
    shared fixtures, semantic mutations against a load-bearing level (dropping level 3's
    support leaves 88 of 127 claims unverifiable), v2 grammar strictness, and thread- and
    shard-invariance of the verdict.
  * The selftest is the regression that guards the quotients: for every state with at most
    three parts, n <= 6 and k <= 4, a deliberately unquotiented naive solver written from
    docs/problem.md decides solvability, and the audit engine must verify exactly the
    unsolvable ones and refuse the solvable ones even given complete support.  That is the
    control shape that caught the 2026-08-31 quotient-composition trap.

Cost of the whole exercise: two aborted EC2 launches (~$0.85, both my pipeline bugs - a
cloud-init HOME fault and a 3-hour job with no progress output), one completed k=7 run
(~$3.3), and about 40 local CPU-minutes.
