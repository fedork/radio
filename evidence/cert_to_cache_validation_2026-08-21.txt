WHY THIS NEEDED VALIDATION, NOT JUST A CORRECT-LOOKING FORMAT
The sa193 certificate stores unsolvability claims in a compact per-level `part`/`claim`/`fact`
format (radio-negative-level-certificate-v2, consumed by radio_refute.c / check_level_chain.py),
not the plain-text "can't solve Sb(...) in k ..." lines a raw solver log emits. radio_oracle.c's
`load` command expects the DISTILLED cache-line format parse_out.sh produces from raw logs:
`- b n1 m1 n2 m2 ... t <pairs> <n> <k>`, fed straight into radiobase.c's cache()/cacheCantSolve().

Two real risks, not hypothetical: (1) a wrong format crashes the loader -- safe, loud, easy to
catch; (2) a wrong PART ORDER within a state could silently insert facts at the wrong place in the
dominance trie, since cacheCantSolve descends the trie edge-by-edge keyed on each part in sequence.
Reading radiobase.c settled the theoretical question -- cacheCantSolve explicitly rotates every
remaining part into the pivot position at each recursion level ("other part permutations in this
call may still add information"), unlike cacheCanSolve, which does assume sort1's descending
order. Since every certificate claim is a NEGATIVE fact, only cacheCantSolve's ordering behavior
applies, and it is permutation-robust by construction. But per this repo's own standard, reading
the source is not the same as trusting it live -- hence the two-scale empirical check below.

METHOD
For each cert level, generate a control population the certificate itself doesn't cover: take a
sample of certified-unsolvable states, perturb one coin on one random part (+-1, and at k=5 also
+-2), and solve each perturbed state FRESH on an oracle with NOTHING loaded -- this is
ground truth, independent of the certificate and the converter entirely. Then load the certificate
into a clean oracle process and re-query both (a) every certified claim -- must come back
UNSOLVABLE -- and (b) every perturbed neighbor -- must match its independently-computed ground
truth exactly. A neighbor mismatch in either direction (a truly solvable neighbor now reporting
unsolvable, or vice versa) would be the signature of trie corruption bleeding into states the
certificate never made a claim about.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH
* Validates the converter and the load *mechanism* at two real scales, with a real (not
  rubber-stamp) control population spanning both solvable and unsolvable ground truth. This is
  meaningfully stronger evidence than "the format looks right" or "the loader didn't crash."
* Does NOT by itself validate the full 8-level, 2,846,568-claim chain -- k=6 through k=9 are
  untested by this note. The mechanism (cacheCantSolve's permutation robustness) doesn't change
  with level, and k=5 is a meaningful majority-share level (80,634 of 2,846,568 claims, and the
  level immediately below the largest, k=7's 2,508,278), so this is reasonable but not complete
  evidence for the whole chain.
* The certificate's own correctness (that these states really are unsolvable) is not what this
  validates -- that rests on the proof-safe cold run9 execution and the certificate's own
  structural/semantic checks (check_level_chain.py, run9_refute). This note only validates that
  the CONVERSION into the oracle's cache format, and the CACHE INSERTION itself, preserve that
  correctness without corrupting anything nearby.
* Reproduce: tools/cert_to_cache.py <cert.zst> > facts.txt, then load into any radio_oracle sized
  for the level's states, with an independent (uncached) oracle process supplying ground truth for
  a perturbed-neighbor control set before trusting the load.
